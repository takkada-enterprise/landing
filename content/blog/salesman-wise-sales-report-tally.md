## Why Distributors Track Sales by Salesman

A distributor with a field team lives with a blind spot. The van goes out, orders come back, stock moves, but who actually drove the numbers is hard to see from the daily total. One route looks strong, but is that the salesman working it or the two big retailers on it who would order anyway.

Tracking sales by salesman closes that gap. It turns a vague sense of "Ramesh is doing well, Suresh has slowed" into a ranked figure the owner can act on. It also changes the conversation with the team, because a review built on a number lands differently from one built on a hunch. For a business paying salaries and incentives to a field force, this is the report that tells you whether the money is working.

## How Tally Captures the Salesman on a Sale

Tally does not have a built-in "salesman" master, so distributors tag it one of a few ways. The most common is cost centres: each salesman is a cost centre, and every sale is allocated to the rep who made it. Some use a voucher-level field or a class, others maintain the salesman as a dimension the invoice carries.

Whichever way it is set, the tag is what makes the report possible. From there, cost centre reports under Display, then Statements of Accounts, then Cost Centres, total sales by salesman for the period, and you can rank them. The discipline is at data-entry time: if the salesman is not tagged on the voucher, the report cannot attribute the sale, so the tag has to be a habit, not an afterthought.

## What the Report Tells an Owner About the Team

Once the sales are attributed, the report answers a set of questions no route sheet can.

- Who is selling the most, and whether that lead is holding or slipping month to month
- A salesman who has gone flat, caught early enough to coach before a quarter is lost
- Whether a strong territory is a strong rep or just a strong set of accounts he inherited
- How incentives should be set, because now they rest on attributed numbers instead of impressions

This is the backbone of running a field team, and it connects directly to the tools the reps carry. A [salesman app on Tally](/blog/salesman-app-tally-india/) that lets a rep invoice and collect in the field is what generates clean, attributed data in the first place.

## Pairing Sales With What the Salesman Collected

Sales attributed to a salesman is one number. What that salesman actually collected is the number that pays the bills. A rep can book heavy sales and leave a trail of overdue accounts, which looks like performance until the receivables report says otherwise.

Read together, sales and collection tell the real story: a rep who sells well and collects well is your best, while a rep who sells well and collects poorly is loading working capital risk onto the business. Setting a salesman's selling next to the [party-wise sales](/blog/party-wise-sales-report-tally/) on his route, and against what those parties owe, is how an owner judges the whole job, not just the top line.

**Q: What is the difference between salesman-wise and party-wise sales reports?**

A: A salesman-wise report attributes sales to the rep who made them; a party-wise report attributes sales to the retailer who bought. An owner reads both together, because a route's numbers can come from a strong salesman or from a couple of strong accounts, and only the two views side by side separate the two.
