The question of WhatsApp Business API own number vs third party comes down to one thing: whose name your retailer sees when the reminder lands. Every business-initiated message on the WhatsApp Business Platform goes out from a registered number, and that number belongs to somebody. If it belongs to the software vendor, your reminder arrives from a stranger's number under a display name that is not your firm, in a fresh chat window your buyer has never opened. If it belongs to you, it drops into the same thread where he already sends you "bhai, kal kar denge". The catch is that a number registered on the Platform can no longer be used in the ordinary WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business phone app, every message template needs Meta's approval before it can be sent, and Meta bills per delivered template at rates that vary by category and country. This article covers the three tiers you might be sold and which one a distributor should actually pick.

## Key Highlights

- There are three separate products with confusingly similar names: the consumer WhatsApp app, the free WhatsApp Business app, and the WhatsApp Business Platform (the API). Only the third one lets software send on your behalf at scale
- Most tools put you on a number they own or rent, because it is faster to set up and because they only have to get one number through Meta's checks instead of one per customer
- The number sending your reminders is a business asset. It decides whether the message lands in a saved contact's thread, what display name shows, and who keeps the number if you change tools
- Running your own number is not free convenience. That number stops working inside the WhatsApp app on a handset, templates need approval before they send, and Meta charges per delivered message

## WhatsApp Business API Own Number vs Third Party, Side by Side

Both routes run on the same Meta infrastructure. The difference is whose number is registered on it.

| | Provider's number | Your own number |
|---|---|---|
| What the retailer sees | An unfamiliar number, often shared with other businesses | Your firm's number, usually already saved in his phone |
| Display name | The provider's, or a generic sender name | Your firm's approved display name |
| Setup effort for you | Almost none | You hand over a number and go through Meta's checks |
| If you leave the tool | The number was never yours. It stays | The number stays yours, and moves with you |
| Replies | Land in the provider's system, if replies are supported at all | Land on your number, in the same thread as everything else |
| Cost | Usually built into the tool's price | Per-message charges plus whatever the tool charges to run it |

Neither column is wrong. A distributor sending a first batch of reminders to see whether they work at all should not be dealing with Meta paperwork. A distributor whose 200 retailers already have his number saved is throwing away his biggest delivery advantage by sending from somebody else's.

## The Three Tiers You Might Be Sold

The names overlap enough that salespeople blur them, sometimes without meaning to.

**The consumer WhatsApp app.** What everyone has. One account per number, chats on the handset. Nothing external can send through it in a supported way, and tools claiming otherwise are automating an unofficial route that gets numbers banned.

**The free WhatsApp Business app.** A separate free app with a business profile, catalogue, labels, quick replies and away messages, built for one person on one phone. Broadcast lists cap at 256 contacts each, and a broadcast only reaches recipients who already have your number saved in their address book. Everyone else is silently skipped. For a distributor with 30 parties and one person doing the follow-up, this is genuinely enough.

**The WhatsApp Business Platform.** Meta's API, and what any software integration uses. No 256-contact list, messages can be triggered by your systems, and business-initiated messages must go as pre-approved templates. It is also the only tier where a per-message charge applies. Every tool offering [automated payment reminders from Tally](/blog/automate-payment-reminders-tally/) or [invoice dispatch on WhatsApp](/blog/tally-whatsapp-invoice-dispatch/) runs on this tier, whatever number it uses.

## What a Business Solution Provider Actually Is

You cannot walk up to Meta and buy API access the way you buy a SIM. Access is intermediated by companies Meta authorises for the purpose, called Business Solution Providers, or Solution Partners in Meta's newer wording. They handle registration, the technical connection, template submission and billing.

Every WhatsApp feature in every business tool you have been pitched sits behind one of these providers. That is normal plumbing. What is worth understanding is why so many tools default to putting you on their number rather than yours.

Three reasons. Registering a number means freeing it from the WhatsApp app, submitting a display name, and working through business verification, which is real work repeated for every customer. Meta's messaging limits and quality scores also attach to the number, so a provider running one well-behaved number can start everyone at a usable send volume instead of at the bottom tier. Both of those are legitimate engineering. The third reason is that a number you never owned cannot follow you out, and that one is worth naming out loud before you sign.

## What Changes When the Message Comes From Your Number

Four concrete differences, in the order a distributor feels them.

**The saved-contact effect.** Your retailers have had your number in their phone for years, filed under your firm name. A message from it arrives inside an existing conversation, next to the rate list you sent last month. A message from an unknown number arrives in a cold thread with block and report buttons sitting right there. Same words, different odds of being read.

**Display name and verification.** On the Platform you submit a display name and Meta approves it. Until the business behind it is verified, contacts who have not saved you see only the raw number. Once verification goes through, your firm's name shows even to people who never saved it. On a provider's number you inherit whatever name they registered.

**Who owns the number if you leave.** The number and its WhatsApp Business Account sit inside a Meta business portfolio, so ask whose portfolio yours will be in before you start. A number under your own portfolio can be pointed at a different provider later, and the migration generally carries the number, display name, quality rating, messaging tier and approved templates across. Chat history does not travel.

**Message history and replies.** When a retailer replies "kar diya payment", that reply goes to whichever number sent the message. On your own number it lands in your thread with the rest of the relationship. On a shared number it lands in a system you do not control, and the conversation lives in two places. This matters when you send [ledger statements on WhatsApp](/blog/how-to-share-ledger-statement-whatsapp-tally/) and the party replies asking about an entry from March.

## What Your Own Number Really Costs

The bill is the smallest part of it. Four things to plan for.

**The number leaves your phone.** This is the one that surprises people. Once a number is registered on the Platform it can no longer be used in WhatsApp Messenger or the WhatsApp Business app. Meta is explicit that the number still works for calls and SMS, but WhatsApp on that handset is gone for that number. So do not register the owner's personal number or the one your counter staff answer chats on. Take a second SIM the firm already owns and give it a display name retailers will recognise.

**Template approval.** Anything sent outside an open 24-hour reply window has to be a template Meta approved in advance, categorised as marketing, utility or authentication. A payment reminder carrying a bill number, amount and due date reads as utility. A scheme announcement reads as marketing. Reviews are usually quick, and rejections are usually about a mismatched category or a malformed placeholder. Edit and resubmit.

**Per-message pricing.** Since 1 July 2025 Meta charges per template message delivered rather than per 24-hour conversation. What you pay depends on the template category and the recipient's country, and utility messages cost substantially less than marketing ones in India. Utility templates sent while a customer service window is already open are not charged. Meta can revise rates up to four times a year, on 1 January, 1 April, 1 July and 1 October, which is why you should not build a business case on a paisa figure read off a blog, including this one. Ask your provider for the current India rate card in writing.

**Quality rating and sending limits.** Meta scores each number on how recipients react, mostly blocks and reports. New business portfolios start at a 250-recipient daily limit, step up to 2,000 after business verification or after 2,000 delivered messages to unique users in 30 days on high-quality templates, then rise through 10,000 and 100,000 to unlimited as volume and quality hold. Let the rating fall and the tier stops climbing or drops. This rewards the distributor who sends a specific reminder about a specific overdue bill and punishes the one blasting a festive greeting at every party in the ledger.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is the difference between the WhatsApp Business app and the WhatsApp Business API?**

A: The WhatsApp Business app is a free app a person installs on a phone, with a business profile, labels and broadcast lists capped at 256 contacts each. The WhatsApp Business Platform, commonly called the API, is what software connects to. It has no 256-contact list, it can be triggered by your systems, and business-initiated messages must go as templates Meta approved in advance. The app is free, the Platform is charged per delivered template message.

**Q: Can I use my own WhatsApp number in the app after registering it on the API?**

A: No. Once a number is registered on the WhatsApp Business Platform it can no longer be used in WhatsApp Messenger or the WhatsApp Business app. Meta confirms the number still works normally for calls and SMS, but WhatsApp on that handset is gone for that number. This is why you register a second SIM the firm owns rather than the owner's personal number or the number your counter staff use for chats.

**Q: Why do most tools send from their own number instead of mine?**

A: Because registering a number is work repeated for every customer, and because a provider running one well-scored number can start everyone at a usable sending volume rather than at Meta's lowest tier. The third reason is that a number you never owned cannot leave with you, so ask whose Meta business portfolio your number will sit under before you commit.

**Q: Do I need approval for every WhatsApp message I send to a customer?**

A: For any business-initiated message outside an open 24-hour reply window, yes. It has to be a template Meta approved beforehand, submitted under a category such as utility, marketing or authentication. Once approved you send it with different values filled in each time, so you approve the shape of the message rather than every individual send.
