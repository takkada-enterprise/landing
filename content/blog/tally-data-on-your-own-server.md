## Where Your Tally Data Already Lives

Start with what is true today, before any app enters the picture. Tally Prime is installed on a Windows machine in your office, and your company data file sits on that same machine or on a drive you control on your own network. Every voucher, every party ledger, every year of history is physically on hardware you own and can walk over to.

That does not change when you put Tally on a phone. As the piece on [connecting an app to Tally safely](/blog/is-it-safe-to-connect-app-to-tally/) lays out, a companion app reads a working slice of your data through the channel Tally already uses to share data with other programs. The master copy stays exactly where it is. If you switched the app off tomorrow, your Tally file would be untouched on the same machine it has always been on.

## What Leaves Your Office on the Standard Plans

On the standard Takkada plans, the app needs a way to show your outstanding, ledgers, and registers on a phone that is not sitting inside your office network. So a copy of the operational data the phone needs is synced through a hosted connection, held in an India region, and that is the copy your salesman sees on the road.

This is the ordinary shape of almost every mobile-for-Tally product, and it is worth being plain about it rather than pretending the phone reads your office desktop directly over the counter. The mechanics of how a phone reaches your books are covered in [how to access Tally on mobile](/blog/how-to-access-tally-on-mobile-step-by-step/), and the fuller storage and access answer sits in [where your Tally data is stored](/blog/where-is-my-tally-data-stored-takkada/). Your company file does not leave the office. A working slice of it travels to the phone so the field team can act.

## When an Own-Server Deployment Is Worth Asking About

For the majority of distributors running ₹2 crore to ₹200 crore of turnover, the standard hosted connection is the right and simpler answer, and the own-server route adds cost and IT overhead they do not need. It becomes worth a conversation when a specific condition is in play: a group-level data policy that requires everything on owned infrastructure, a compliance obligation from a large principal, or an internal IT team that already runs private servers and prefers to keep this there too.

If that is you, the honest next step is to ask rather than assume. Tell us the constraint, and we will tell you what an own-server deployment would involve. For everyone else, the reassuring part stays the same, and it is the part that matters most: whichever route you take, Tally stays your system of record, and the hosted [tally cloud](/blog/tally-cloud/) discussion covers the same trade-off for the underlying Tally file itself.
