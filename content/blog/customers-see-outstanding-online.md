## Can My Customers See Their Outstanding Online, and What the Link Shows Him

You pick the party in the app and share the link, usually on the same WhatsApp thread where the orders come in. He taps it and the page opens on his phone with his name at the top and his open bills listed under it.

| What he sees on the link | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Each open invoice with its voucher number | Your Tally sales voucher |
| Amount still outstanding on that bill | The bill after every receipt already allocated to it |
| Due status per bill: due in N days, due today, or N days overdue | The credit terms on that bill |
| A tick box per bill, or select all | His choice of what to clear now |
| A custom amount box | For a part payment he wants to make |

He ticks what he intends to pay, or types an amount, and pays by UPI at 0% MDR. The receipt is written back into Tally against the specific bills he selected, which is Tally's own bill-by-bill allocation rather than a lump sitting on account. If your receipts have historically gone in on account, the difference this makes is the whole subject of [bill by bill against reference](/blog/bill-by-bill-against-reference-tally/), and it is what keeps every outstanding figure downstream honest.

The page is a working surface for one party's pending bills, and it stops there.

## Why a Retailer Calls to Ask His Own Balance

Distributors treat this call as a nuisance. It is worth understanding why it happens, because the reason decides whether a link actually removes the call or just moves it.

**He does not trust his own book.** Most retail counters record purchases late and record them from the physical bill, not from your ledger. His munim enters things in the order the papers surface. So when he needs a number before paying you, his own figure is stale and he knows it. Calling you is faster than auditing himself.

**He is checking whether you noticed his payment.** He transferred money last Thursday, nobody confirmed anything, and he wants to know it landed against the right bill before he sends more. This is the same anxiety that makes distributors send a [partywise outstanding statement](/blog/partywise-outstanding-statement-tally/) at month end, except the retailer wants it on the day he pays, not on the 30th.

**He wants to know what is overdue before your salesman arrives.** He is deciding what to pay and what to push, and he would rather do that quietly on his phone than in front of your man at the counter.

Only the first two disappear the moment he can see the list himself. The third one never disappears, and it should not, because a retailer planning his payments against your due dates is a retailer who pays.

## What Changes When He Is Looking at the Same Open Bills You Are

The change that distributors notice first is that one specific argument stops happening.

Today the sequence runs like this. He pays a round figure, say ₹40,000, against a party balance made up of four bills. Nobody on either side says which bills that ₹40,000 was meant for. Your accountant allocates it his own way, oldest first. Two weeks later the retailer says the March bill was cleared, your statement says the March bill is open, and both of you are correct inside your own books. Now somebody has to sit with two sets of paper and rebuild an intention that was never recorded.

When he pays from the link, he ticks the bills. The intention is captured at the instant the money moves, and the receipt reaches Tally carrying that allocation. There is nothing left to reconstruct, so there is nothing left to argue about. The same discipline is what makes a UPI collection on an invoice reconcile cleanly, which is covered in [accepting online payment on a Tally invoice](/blog/accept-online-payment-on-tally-invoice/).

The second change is quieter. When the retailer can see his own overdue count, the conversation your salesman has at the counter starts from an agreed number instead of a disputed one. Half of collection friction is two people arguing about the input rather than the payment.

## The Two Objections Every Distributor Raises

In every demo, the same two questions come up before anything else, and both deserve a straight answer.

**"Will he see something he should not?"** He sees his own bills. The link is issued per party, and the page is built from that party's open invoices only. There is no dealer list, no other retailer's balance, no rate for anybody else, no stock position, and no view of your books. A distributor's real fear here is usually competitive, which is that one retailer learns what another retailer owes or gets billed at. That information is not on the page in any form.

**"If I send a link today, does it stay live forever?"** No. The link carries a token and the token expires. A message forwarded onward months later, or a screenshot circulated after the relationship has soured, does not keep opening a live view of that party's account. When you want him to see the current position again, you send a fresh link, which is the same motion as sending the statement anyway.

Those two properties are what let you send the link on a group thread without thinking twice about it.

## What the Link Deliberately Cannot Do

Being clear about the boundary matters more than adding to the list, because a distributor who expects one thing and gets another stops trusting the whole product.

The retailer cannot log in. There is no account, no password, no permanent address he can bookmark and check whenever he feels like it. The link reaches him because you sent it.

He cannot browse his history. The page carries what is open right now. Paid and closed bills from last quarter are not there for him to page through, and neither is his purchase register.

He cannot download his own statement. There is no self-service report on his side. If he wants the full ledger for a period, he asks you and you send it.

There is nothing on the vendor side either. This surface exists for the party who owes you money, and it does not extend to the suppliers you owe.

If what you actually want is a permanent login where a customer signs in and inspects his account whenever he likes, that does not exist here, and saying so is more useful than a demo that implies otherwise.

## Where the Ledger Statement Still Fits

The link answers "what do I owe you right now". A ledger statement answers "show me every entry between these two dates", and that is a different request, usually made by his accountant at year end or during a reconciliation.

That one stays a send from your side. The statement generates as a PDF and goes out on WhatsApp, which is walked through in [sharing a ledger statement on WhatsApp from Tally](/blog/how-to-share-ledger-statement-whatsapp-tally/). It is deliberate that this is pull-on-request rather than always available to him: a full ledger is a different level of disclosure from a list of pending bills, and most distributors want to decide, party by party, when to hand it over.

Between the two, the everyday call gets answered by the link and the once-a-year reconciliation gets answered by the PDF.
