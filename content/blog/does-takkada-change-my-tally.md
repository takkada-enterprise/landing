## What "Touch My Data" Actually Means

When a distributor or his accountant asks whether an app will touch their Tally data, they are picturing a specific nightmare: the app reaching into last quarter's ledgers, quietly changing a balance, deleting a voucher, or corrupting a year-end close that took days to get right. That fear is reasonable, because the books are the business, and it is the right instinct to protect them.

So it helps to separate two very different actions. One is adding a new entry, the invoice you just raised or the payment you just collected. The other is altering an existing entry, going back and changing something already in the books. Almost all the anxiety about an app "touching" Tally is really about the second kind. Once you see that Takkada does the first and not the second, most of the worry settles. The broader question of app safety, read scope, and control is covered in [is it safe to connect an app to Tally](/blog/is-it-safe-to-connect-app-to-tally/).

## What Takkada Writes Into Tally, and What It Never Does

The cleanest way to answer "will the app modify my Tally data" is to draw the line exactly, because the difference between what it writes and what it leaves alone is the whole answer.

| Action | Does Takkada do this |
|---|---|
| Add a sales invoice you raise on the phone | Yes, as a new voucher |
| Post a UPI receipt you collect against an open invoice | Yes, as a new voucher |
| Update a party balance because of an entry you made | Yes, the way any new voucher would |
| Edit a historical voucher you did not touch | No |
| Change or overwrite an existing balance on its own | No |
| Delete vouchers or ledgers by itself | No |

Everything in the "yes" column is an entry you initiated, and it lands where you can see it. Everything in the "no" column is the silent editing owners are afraid of, and it does not happen. The app adds; it does not rewrite.

## How a Phone Action Becomes a Tally Voucher

The reason it works this way is the [two-way Tally sync](/blog/bidirectional-tally-sync-explained/) underneath. Data flows out of Tally to keep the phone current, and when you act on the phone, that one action flows back as a proper voucher.

Walk through a real moment. Your salesman stands at a retailer's counter and raises a GST invoice on the phone. That invoice is written into Tally as a sales voucher, the same voucher your accountant would have typed, so nobody re-keys it at 9 PM. Later the retailer pays on a UPI link, and that receipt posts back into Tally against the open invoice. That matching is the basis of [auto-reconciliation](/blog/auto-reconciliation-tally/), where a payment becomes a clean reconciled receipt rather than a duplicate or a guess. In both cases the app created a new entry because you took an action. It did not go looking for old entries to change.

## Why Your Existing Books and History Stay Safe

The continuity is the point for a business that has trusted Tally for years, so it is worth stating what does not change. Your historical vouchers stay exactly as they were posted. Your year-end closes stay intact. The reports your accountant runs at month-end still run off Tally, off the same numbers, because Tally remains the system of record and no history is migrated away.

If the app and Tally ever disagreed, Tally would win, because Tally is the record and the app is the layer on top of it. That hierarchy is deliberate, and it is what lets an accountant who guards the books for a living add a phone to the workflow without lying awake about it.
