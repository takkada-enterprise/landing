Daily wage salary calculation is paid days multiplied by the day rate. Paid days are the days the worker actually worked plus any approved paid leave. A godown worker on ₹550 a day with 7 paid days earns 7 × ₹550 = ₹3,850. Over a month you add overtime at the agreed rate and take away any advance being recovered. The part that causes arguments is Sundays and holidays. Many businesses pay daily-rated workers only for days worked and paid leave, so a weekly off or a festival holiday earns nothing. Others pay the weekly off as well, especially for workers who come in all six days. Which one applies to you depends on your agreement with the worker and your state's rules on weekly holidays and minimum wages, so check with your CA before you settle on it. The worked example below runs one godown worker through November 2026 both ways, then shows piece-rate pay as the other common option for daily workers.

## Key Highlights

- Daily wage pay = (days worked + approved paid leave) × day rate, so 22 paid days at ₹550 come to ₹12,100.
- Whether Sundays and holidays are paid depends on your agreement and your state's rules; in the example, paying them adds 6 × ₹550 = ₹3,300 to the month.
- Piece-rate pay is pieces made × rate per piece, such as 140 dozen brackets polished at ₹18 a dozen for ₹2,520.

## The formula

| Part | How it is worked out |
|---|---|
| Paid days | Days worked + approved paid leave (+ weekly offs and holidays, if you pay them) |
| Wages | Paid days × day rate |
| Overtime | Overtime hours × hourly rate × the overtime multiple |
| Advance recovered | The agreed instalment for the month |
| Net pay | Wages + overtime + any bonus − deductions − advance recovered |

The hourly rate for a daily worker is the day rate divided by the hours in the normal working day. At ₹550 for 8 hours, that is ₹68.75 an hour.

## November for one godown worker, worked out

Anil loads trucks at ₹550 a day. November 2026 has 30 days, five Sundays and, in this example, one company holiday on a weekday. That leaves 24 working days. Anil worked 21 of them, took 1 day of approved paid leave and was absent for 2. He also put in 6 hours of overtime and is repaying a ₹2,000 advance at ₹1,000 a month.

| Line | Days worked and paid leave only | Sundays and holiday also paid |
|---|---|---|
| Paid days | 21 + 1 = 22 | 22 + 5 + 1 = 28 |
| Wages at ₹550 | ₹12,100 | ₹15,400 |
| Overtime, 6 hours × ₹68.75 × 2 | ₹825 | ₹825 |
| Advance recovered | − ₹1,000 | − ₹1,000 |
| **Net pay** | **₹11,925** | **₹15,225** |

The two absent days are unpaid either way. The ₹3,300 gap between the columns is the six Sundays and holiday, which is why it is worth deciding the rule before the first month rather than at the counter.

For a single week the sum is shorter. If Anil worked 6 days and had 1 day of paid leave, that is 7 paid days × ₹550 = ₹3,850.

## Sundays and holidays: two common practices

**Paying for days worked and paid leave only.** The day rate is the whole deal: no work, no wage. It is simple to count from attendance. It suits casual workers who come and go.

**Paying the weekly off too.** Some businesses pay the weekly off to daily workers who have worked the full week, and pay national and festival holidays as well. This can come from the state's rules, from the minimum wage notification that applies to the work, or simply from the owner's own practice.

Weekly holidays, festival holidays and minimum wages are set by a mix of central law (the Code on Wages, 2019 and the labour codes in force since 21 November 2025) and state rules, and they vary by state and by type of work. We are not giving legal advice here. If you pay for the weekly off, it is often simpler to put that worker on a monthly salary instead, so the month covers the offs on its own.

## Paid leave, overtime and advances

**Paid leave** counts as a paid day once you approve it, provided the worker has leave left for the year.

**Overtime** for work beyond the normal working day is paid at not less than twice the normal rate of wages under section 14 of the Code on Wages, 2019. At ₹550 for 8 hours, that is ₹137.50 for each overtime hour. Our [overtime calculation formula](/blog/overtime-calculation-formula-india/) guide covers weekly-off overtime and late fines.

**Advances** are recovered in the agreed instalments. Write the amount taken back and the balance left on the payslip so the worker can see where the money went.

## Piece-rate pay as the other option

Piece-rate workers are paid for output, not days: pieces made × rate per piece. A polisher who finishes 6 dozen brackets in a day at ₹18 a dozen earns ₹108 that day. Over a month of 140 dozen, that is ₹2,520. Keep the count by work type, because different jobs carry different rates. Under the Code on Wages, piece-rate workers should still not fall below the minimum time rate for their work, so compare a month's piece pay with the minimum wage that applies.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Are daily wage workers paid for Sundays?**

A: It depends on your agreement with the worker and your state's rules on weekly holidays and minimum wages. Many businesses pay daily-rated workers only for days worked and paid leave, while others pay the weekly off too. In the example, paying five Sundays and one holiday adds ₹3,300 to a ₹550-a-day worker's month, so settle the rule with your CA first.

**Q: How do you calculate daily wage for a month?**

A: Count the paid days (days worked plus approved paid leave, plus offs and holidays if you pay them) and multiply by the day rate. Then add overtime and take away any advance being recovered. A worker with 22 paid days at ₹550, 6 overtime hours and a ₹1,000 recovery gets ₹11,925.

**Q: How is piece rate salary calculated?**

A: Multiply the pieces made by the rate for that piece or work type, and add up the month. 140 dozen at ₹18 a dozen is ₹2,520. Keep a daily count per work type, and check that the month's piece pay does not fall below the minimum wage for that work.

**Q: Do daily wage workers get paid leave?**

A: They can, if you give it. Many businesses allow a set number of paid leave days a year to regular daily workers, and an approved paid leave day is paid at the day rate, ₹550 in the example. State rules on leave can apply as well, so check with your CA.
