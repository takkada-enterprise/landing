## How do I stop my salesman from seeing my Tally reports?

Turn the Reports tab off for that team member. That is the whole control on the phone. He then does not see reports at all: not Trial Balance, not Profit & Loss, not Collections, not Daybook, not Sales Analytics, not Customer Analytics. The permission is per member. It applies to the tab.

Raju is on the Fancy Bazaar beat. He does not need the 12-month MIS grid, and he does not need the company's Profit & Loss at 9 PM in a tea stall. The Reports tab is how the owner keeps those screens on his own phone. The working-day list of what the owner himself opens is the [daily checklist for Tally reports](/blog/tally-reports-for-distributors-daily-checklist/).

The Tally desktop story of security levels, and of rights that attach to a report type, is [how to restrict salesman access in Tally](/blog/restrict-salesman-access-tally/). This post is the phone tab.

## Restrict reports access tally for one team member

Restrict reports access tally by opening that member and switching the Reports permission off. One member, one switch. Raju's Reports tab stays off, while the accountant and the owner keep theirs on. A second salesman, same as Raju, off.

With the tab off, the member does not see the Reports section. Financial Statements (Trial Balance, Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, MIS Reports) stay out of reach. Action Reports (Collections, Bills Receivable, Bills Payable, Sales Analytics, Customer Analytics, Cross-sell Gaps, Inventory, Purchase Analytics, Daybook) stay out of reach. The This Financial Year band of ten register totals stays out of reach with them.

With the tab on, the member sees that whole Reports section. Party search is a search box. He types a name. There is no salesman filter on the reports, and no beat chip. Godown is its own report.

That is the honest shape. The owner who wants Raju off Reports turns the permission off. The owner who wants the accountant on Reports leaves it on. Two members, two settings.

## What stays closed when Reports is off

Raju's phone does not show the Reports section. Outstanding at 8:30, Daybook at noon, Sales Analytics at 9 PM, Trial Balance on the second: those sittings happen on the owner's device, or on the accountant's, for members who have the tab on.

Daybook of today's sales vouchers still exists. It opens on the owner's phone, Today, voucher-type chip on Sales, which is how "12 bill ho gaye" at 12:10 becomes seven vouchers, on [Daybook from Tally on the phone](/blog/daybook-tally-mobile/). Raju does not open Daybook himself if Reports is off.

Sharma's ₹1,85,000 is still a number the owner checks at 8:30 on Outstanding. The hold on mustard oil is a sentence the owner sends. It does not come from a Profit & Loss Raju opened in the van, because that screen is behind the tab.

The five-report habit an owner actually uses on a phone is written up as [the five reports an owner opens](/blog/view-tally-reports-on-mobile/). That habit belongs to the people with Reports on.

## What the owner still opens at 8:30

Month-end, Trial Balance for the CA, on [Trial Balance from Tally on the phone](/blog/trial-balance-tally-mobile/). All of that is the owner's tab.

The wider set of [Tally reports on mobile](/tally-reports-on-mobile) is how the phone reads the same Tally books. The permission decides who opens that set. The set stays one set. On or off, per member.

If the accountant needs Trial Balance on the second of the month while the owner is at Fancy Bazaar, the accountant's member has Reports on. If a new salesman joins next week, his member starts from whatever the owner sets. Copy Raju's setting if he is on the same kind of beat.

## Tally desktop security, and this tab

Tally on the desktop has security levels. You can disallow a report type for a level, so a data-entry user never opens the Purchase Register. That is a desktop control, and it is real, and it is the one [restrict salesman access in Tally](/blog/restrict-salesman-access-tally/) walks through. The phone Reports tab does not copy that shape. It is the tab, per member, on or off.

Owners ask, in the same breath, whether Raju can open Daybook and skip Profit & Loss. On this phone the answer is the tab. Daybook and Profit & Loss live behind the same Reports permission. Turning the tab on for Raju gives him the section. Turning it off gives him none of it.

There is no salesman filter on the reports for the people who do have the tab. The owner typing Sharma in Outstanding is using a search box. Raju, if he had the tab, would be using the same search box. That is why most owners keep Raju off the tab and keep Outstanding on their own phone at 8:30.

## Sharing a report the salesman should not see

The CA asks for a Trial Balance. The owner exports PDF or Excel from his own phone, then the share sheet, then WhatsApp or email. Raju is not in that thread. The file left the owner's device at 9:20, still at Fancy Bazaar, the way the Trial Balance sitting already works on the second of the month. The [Tally-format export](/blog/tally-format-export-trial-balance-balance-sheet/) is the layout the CA expects, still from a member who can open Reports.

Sharma's "bhai, statement bhej do" is a party-page send, not a Reports-tab send. Ledger statement and pending-bills statement go from the party page, the bills page and the ageing report. That path is [sharing a ledger statement](/blog/how-to-share-ledger-statement-whatsapp-tally/). Ageing itself sits inside Reports, so the ageing-report send belongs to a member who can open the tab.

Every exported PDF or Excel from a report goes through the phone's share sheet, where you pick WhatsApp, or email, or Drive. The permission question is who can produce the file. The share sheet is how it leaves. Raju does not get a copy of the Trial Balance thread. He was never on Reports.

## When a new salesman joins next week

Tuesday, a second beat opens toward Maligaon. The new member gets a phone. The owner copies Raju's Reports setting: off. The accountant stays on. The owner stays on. Three members, two of them dark on Reports, one of them reading Sharma's ₹1,85,000 at 8:30.

The temptation is to turn the tab on "just for Daybook" so the new man can check his own bills. Daybook lives behind the same tab as Profit & Loss and MIS Reports. Turning it on for the new man gives him the section. The owner who wants him off Profit & Loss keeps him off the tab, and checks Daybook himself at 12:15 the way he already checks Raju's seven-not-twelve.

A month later, if this person moves into the office as a second accountant, the same member can have Reports switched on. The permission follows the member, not the beat. There is still no salesman filter on any report he then opens. He types a name in the search box, like everyone else with the tab. Friday 9 PM, the owner is in the car and the new man is off Reports, so Outstanding for Sharma's ₹1,85,000 is still the owner's sitting. That is the arrangement he wanted when he hired for Maligaon. The tab kept it.
