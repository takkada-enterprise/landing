## What a Party-Wise Sales Report Shows

A daily total tells you the business sold well or badly. A party-wise report tells you who did the buying. It answers the question an owner really carries: of my three hundred retailers, which ones are actually driving the numbers, and which have gone soft this month.

Most distribution businesses follow the same shape. A small set of parties buys the bulk of the stock, a wider set buys steadily, and a long tail buys occasionally. The party-wise report makes that shape visible and, more usefully, makes changes in it visible. A top-ten party that bought thirty percent less this month than last is a signal, maybe a competitor got in, maybe the retailer is holding stock, and it is worth a call before it becomes a lost account.

## Where to Build It in Tally

In Tally Prime you have two clean routes. From the Sales Register, change the view or use the columnar option to group sales by party, giving each retailer a period total. Or open a single party ledger through Display, then Account Books, then Ledger, and read that one retailer's buying over the period.

For the ranked, whole-business view, the grouped Sales Register is the one, because you can sort parties by value and see the leaderboard in one screen. Set the period to this month, last month, or a custom range, and the same report re-ranks. It is the sister report to the [daily sales report](/blog/daily-sales-report-tally-mobile/): the daily view is the headline, the party-wise view is who wrote it.

## The Decisions a Distributor Makes From It

The ranking drives where the owner and the field team spend their attention, so it earns a daily look.

- A top party trending down gets a call and a personal visit before the account slips
- A steady mid-tier party that suddenly spikes may be worth a better rate or a credit-limit review
- The long tail that buys once a quarter shapes route planning, so the van does not burn a day on low-value stops
- The concentration itself is a risk read: if three parties are forty percent of sales, losing one hurts, and the owner manages those relationships accordingly

These are relationship and route decisions, and they are best made when the owner is out among the parties, not that evening at the desk.

## Pairing Sales With What Each Party Owes

Sales alone is half the picture. A party that buys the most is only your best party if it also pays. The other half is what each retailer owes, and the two read together is where the real judgement lives.

A retailer buying heavily but stretching every payment is quietly funding himself on your working capital, and that shows only when you set his buying next to his [outstanding statement](/blog/partywise-outstanding-statement-tally/). Read side by side, the party-wise sales report and the party-wise outstanding tell you not just who buys the most, but who is worth the credit you extend them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is a party-wise sales report in Tally?**

A: It is your sales broken down by retailer, showing how much each party bought over a chosen period. In Tally Prime you build it from the Sales Register grouped by party, or from a single party's ledger, and you can sort parties high to low to see which retailers drive the most revenue.

**Q: How do I make a party-wise sales report in Tally Prime?**

A: Open the Sales Register from Display More Reports, then Account Books, and use the columnar or grouped view to total sales by party for the period. For one retailer, open its ledger through Display, then Account Books, then Ledger. Setting the period to this month or a custom range re-ranks the parties automatically.

**Q: Can I see party-wise sales on my phone?**

Because it stays in two-way sync with Tally, the ranking matches the office desktop and refreshes as new vouchers post, so you can check who is buying less this month between routes.

**Q: How is a party-wise sales report different from an outstanding report?**

A: A party-wise sales report shows how much each retailer bought. An outstanding report shows how much each retailer still owes. Buying and owing are different questions, and a distributor reads them together, because a party that buys a lot but pays slowly is not the same as one that buys a lot and clears on time.

**Q: Does the party-wise report need bill-wise details switched on?**

A: No. The sales ranking works from the sales vouchers themselves, so you get party totals without bill-wise tracking. You do want bill-wise details on if you also want the outstanding and ageing for the same parties, because that is what ties each unpaid amount to a specific bill and its due date.
