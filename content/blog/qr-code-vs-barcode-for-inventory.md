On QR code vs barcode for inventory, the practical answer for most distributors is the line barcode, because it works with the cheapest USB gun at the billing desk and holds everything a stock sticker needs: the item and the quantity in the box. A QR code holds far more data, reads from any angle and survives some damage, but many low-cost laser and CCD guns cannot read it at all, so choosing QR can mean replacing every scanner. QR earns its place when the label has to carry a lot, such as a web link, a full batch record or a serial number list, or when every scan is done by phone camera or a 2D imager gun. For billing and stock movement in a godown, a Code 128 barcode on a carton sticker does the job on any scanner you already own. Below is the comparison side by side, the scanner question that usually decides it, and when a QR code is the right call.

## Side by side

| | Line barcode (Code 128, EAN-13) | QR code |
|---|---|---|
| Data it holds | A short code, a few dozen characters at most | Hundreds of characters, including links |
| Cheap USB laser or CCD gun | Reads it | Cannot read it |
| 2D imager gun | Reads it | Reads it |
| Phone camera | Reads it | Reads it |
| Scan angle | Across the bars | Any angle |
| Damaged label | Fails if the bars are cut | Often still reads |
| Print space | Wide and short | Small square |

## The scanner question decides it

Count what your team already scans with. A billing desk with a laser gun, two godown boys with CCD guns and a salesman with a phone can all read a line barcode. Switch the stickers to QR and the laser and CCD guns stop working, so you buy 2D imager guns for every counter before anyone bills a box.

The phone camera reads both, which is why a business that scans only by phone can go either way. Most distributors mix phones and guns.

## How much data a stock sticker needs

A sticker for billing has to answer two questions: which item, and how many. That fits in a short code. Everything else, the rate, the party, the GST, comes from your books when the scan lands on the bill. Packing a whole product record into a QR code does not help billing, and it goes out of date the day the rate changes.

## When a QR code is the right call

- The label sends a retailer or customer to a web page, such as a product catalogue or a payment link.
- Each piece needs its own serial number or a long batch record.
- The label is tiny and will be scanned only by phone.
- Labels get scuffed in transit, and a QR code's damage tolerance matters more than scanner cost.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is a QR code better than a barcode for inventory?**

A: It holds more and reads from any angle, but many cheap barcode guns cannot read it. For billing and stock stickers that carry an item and a quantity, a line barcode is usually enough.

**Q: Can a normal barcode scanner read QR codes?**

A: Only if it is a 2D imager. Basic laser and CCD guns read line barcodes only. Check the scanner's spec for "2D" before switching.

**Q: Can I scan QR codes with a phone?**

A: Yes. A phone camera reads QR codes and line barcodes alike, which is why phone-only teams can use either.

**Q: Which takes less space on a sticker?**

A: A QR code fits in a small square. A line barcode needs width, plus blank space on both sides, so very small labels favour QR.
