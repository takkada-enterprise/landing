## Why the Purchase Report Matters as Much as Sales

Distributors instinctively track sales and treat purchases as background noise. That is a costly habit, because the buy side sets the two numbers that decide whether the business makes money: the cost that determines margin, and the cash commitment that determines working capital.

A supplier whose rates crept up two percent over a quarter quietly eats margin on every case sold, and nobody notices until the purchase report is read carefully. Buying too much of a slow line ties up cash that a busy season needs. Buying too little of a fast one loses sales at the counter. The purchase report is where all three show up, and reading it with the same attention as the sales report is what separates a distributor who manages his margin from one who only manages his top line.

## Where Purchases Live Inside Tally

In Tally Prime, every purchase you book is a voucher, and the Purchase Register gathers them. From Gateway of Tally, go to Display More Reports, then Account Books, then Purchase Register, and set the period. You get every purchase voucher with its supplier and value, and the period total at the foot.

Group the register by party and it becomes supplier-wise purchases, showing how much you bought from each principal or vendor. Drill into a single supplier ledger and you see the individual bills behind the figure. It is the exact mirror of the [daily sales report](/blog/daily-sales-report-tally-mobile/), pointed at the buy side: same register logic, opposite direction of goods and money.

## Reading Supplier-Wise Buying the Right Way

The consolidated purchase total tells you how much stock came in. The supplier-wise breakdown tells you the things worth acting on.

Read supplier by supplier, you can see which principal you depend on most, whether a supplier's effective rate has drifted, and how your buying is spread across vendors. Sort by value and the concentration is obvious: if one supplier is most of your purchasing, that relationship carries the business and deserves careful terms. Set the period to compare this month against last, and a creeping cost or a swelling order jumps out before it becomes a habit. Read carelessly, as one lump total, all of that stays hidden.

## Purchases, Payables, and the Next Order

Buying and owing are two sides of one decision. What you bought is history; what you still owe on it is the live commitment that shapes your next move. A distributor about to place a fresh order needs both: what he has been buying, and what already falls due this week.

That is why the purchase report reads best next to the payables view. Set your buying against your [accounts payable in Tally](/blog/accounts-payable-in-tally-for-distributors/) and the next order gets decided against real cash commitments, not a rough memory of them. Keeping [purchase bills tracked bill by bill](/blog/purchase-bill-tracking-tally/) is what lets you tie each payment to the bill it settles and reconcile cleanly when a supplier sends his statement.
