## What Sundry Creditors Means in Tally

Sundry Creditors is Tally's default group for the parties you owe money to for goods and services bought on credit. "Sundry" just means assorted, so it is the catch-all bucket for every supplier who is not a bank, a loan, or a statutory dues account. For a distributor this is where the FMCG principal, the local transporter, the carton vendor, and the electricity of the warehouse (once booked and unpaid) all sit.

Because it is a liability group, its balance is money leaving your business in future. The full accounting side of this is covered in [accounts payable in Tally for distributors](/blog/accounts-payable-in-tally-for-distributors/); here the focus is the group itself and how to read it from a phone. The one-line version a distributor would repeat: Sundry Creditors is "jinko humein dena hai," the people we owe.

## How the Group Is Built, Ledger by Ledger

Every supplier you buy from on credit is a separate ledger classified under Sundry Creditors. When you record a purchase voucher, Tally credits that supplier's ledger by the invoice amount, and the supplier's outstanding grows. When you pay, you debit the ledger and the outstanding shrinks. The running total of all those ledgers, rolled up, is the group balance.

This is the same structure as the debtor side, only pointed at who you buy from instead of who you sell to. The relationship between the two groups is laid out in [creditors vs debtors in Tally](/blog/creditors-vs-debtors-tally/). The practical upshot: if a purchase is not showing in your payables, either the ledger was misclassified out of Sundry Creditors, or the purchase voucher was never booked. Both are worth catching early, because a payable you cannot see is a payment you will make late.

## Reading Supplier Dues the Right Way

The number most owners glance at is the group closing balance. That is the total, but it hides timing, which is the thing that actually costs money. To read timing you need bill-wise details switched on for each supplier ledger, so every open purchase bill carries its own due date worked out from the credit period you agreed.

With that on, the payables report becomes a schedule instead of a lump:

| Supplier | Total due | Due this week | Oldest bill |
|---|---|---|---|
| FMCG principal | ₹4,20,000 | ₹1,80,000 | 12 days |
| Transporter | ₹64,000 | ₹64,000 | 8 days |
| Carton vendor | ₹38,500 | ₹0 | 3 days |

Read this way, you pay the ₹64,000 freight due now and the ₹1.8 lakh to the principal on time to protect the cash discount, and you leave the carton vendor to his due date. The same bill-wise discipline that keeps this honest is explained in [bill-wise payables tracking in Tally](/blog/bill-wise-payables-tracking-tally/).

## Keeping the Creditor List Clean

A messy Sundry Creditors group makes every payable read wrong. Two things drift over time. First, duplicate supplier ledgers: "Sharma Traders" and "Sharma Trading Co." for the same vendor, so dues split across two names and neither shows the real total. Second, misclassified ledgers, where an advance or a one-off vendor lands outside the group and quietly disappears from payables.

The fix is boring and it works. One ledger per supplier, named the way the supplier names himself on his invoice, all sitting under Sundry Creditors with bill-wise on. When the list is clean, supplier reconciliation stops being an argument. When the principal sends his statement, your bill-wise creditor ledger lines up against it entry for entry and the two of you agree in minutes. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is Sundry Creditors in Tally?**

A: Sundry Creditors is the Tally group that holds the ledgers of all parties you owe money to for goods and services bought on credit, such as suppliers, principals, and transporters. It is a liability group, and its closing balance is your total accounts payable at any moment. Each supplier is a separate ledger under the group.

**Q: How do I see supplier outstanding in Tally?**

A: Go to Gateway of Tally, then Display More Reports, then Statements of Accounts, then Outstandings, then Payables for the consolidated figure. To see one supplier bill by bill, open the ledger with bill-wise details on. That gives each open purchase bill its own due date so you can sort by what falls due first.

**Q: What is the difference between Sundry Creditors and Sundry Debtors?**

A: Sundry Creditors holds the parties you owe (suppliers), and its balance is a liability. Sundry Debtors holds the parties who owe you (retailers), and its balance is an asset. One is money going out, the other money coming in. A distributor runs both groups at the same time inside one Tally company.

**Q: Can I check my supplier dues on my phone?**

**Q: Why are my sundry creditors showing the wrong total?**

A: Usually one of two reasons: a duplicate supplier ledger splitting dues across two names, or a supplier ledger misclassified out of the Sundry Creditors group so it drops off the payables report. Keep one clean ledger per supplier under the group with bill-wise details on, and the total reconciles against the supplier's own statement.
