## What Takkada Automates, Plainly

These are the jobs where a fixed rule always produces the right result, so a machine can do them the moment the trigger fires.

- **Invoice dispatch.** When an invoice is saved in Tally, its PDF and details go to the retailer's WhatsApp within seconds, no forwarding step.
- **Payment reminders.** As a bill nears or crosses its due date, a reminder goes out on schedule with a UPI link, the tone matched to how overdue it is.
- **UPI collection.** The retailer taps the link and pays on a 0% MDR rail straight to the distributor's own handle.
- **Reconciliation.** When the money lands, the receipt is matched to its open invoice and posted back into Tally as a receipt voucher.
- **Purchase entry from PDF.** A supplier's PDF bill is read into a Tally purchase voucher for the accountant to confirm.

Each of these has its own guide, from [receipts posting back on their own](/blog/hands-free-reconciliation-tally/) to the full [collections loop on autopilot](/blog/tally-collections-on-autopilot/). The common thread is that a person used to type the result, and now the event does.

## What Takkada Does Not Automate, and Why

This is the part most tools skip, and it matters more than the feature list.

Takkada does not make credit decisions. Whether a retailer who is 45 days late gets a fresh order or a hold is a call the owner keeps, because it depends on the relationship and the risk, not on a rule.

Takkada does not resolve disputes. When a retailer says the rate or the quantity is wrong, a human has to check the delivery and decide. The software surfaces the dispute early; it does not settle it.

Takkada does not guess at ambiguous receipts. When one payment could close three different combinations of open bills, [auto-reconciliation](/blog/auto-reconciliation-tally/) flags it for review rather than picking one and corrupting the outstanding report. Roughly a small share of receipts land in this exception queue, and the accountant clears them in a few minutes each morning.

Takkada does not chase for you after the escalation point. A well-overdue account routes to the owner because a phone call from a person, at that stage, is the tool, not another automated message.

Auto Invoice Dispatch is the headline feature of the Full Access plan, and it is available as an add-on on the Collections plan. Import-from-PDF for supplier bills is bundled into Full Access and available as an add-on on Collections. Advanced business reporting comes through the Reports+ add-on. Reminders and UPI collection sit in the collection-focused plans. The [full pricing and plans guide](/blog/takkada-pricing-plans-2026/) lays out which plan carries what, so nobody buys expecting an automation that their plan does not include.

## What Takkada Never Touches in Your Tally

Scope-honesty runs the other way too. There are things Takkada will not do to your data, by design.

It does not edit or delete your existing vouchers. It writes new vouchers (receipts, and purchase entries you confirm) and leaves your history read-only. It does not move your books off Tally or keep a competing copy that drifts. Tally stays the single source of truth, and Takkada reads from it and writes back over a [two-way sync](/blog/bidirectional-tally-sync-explained/). Your accountant's month-end, your ledgers, and your company file all stay exactly as they are. This is the same continuity described in [what Takkada is](/blog/what-is-takkada/): a layer on top of Tally, not a replacement for it.

## How to Think About the Split

The clean way to hold it: Takkada automates the typing, not the thinking. If a task always has one correct answer given the rule (this invoice goes to this WhatsApp number, this UTR settles this bill), it runs on its own. If a task needs judgment (how much credit, which dispute is real, which allocation is right), it comes to a person with the work of gathering the facts already done.

A distributor who understands that split gets the benefit without the surprise. The hours of mechanical entry disappear, the decisions stay where they belong, and Tally keeps being the book everyone trusts.
