## What "Customisable" Really Means for a Distributor

When a distributor asks whether an app can be customised, he usually is not asking for a rebuilt product. He is asking a practical question: will this thing match how my business already runs, or will I have to change my routine to suit it. A paint distributor wants his shade-wise items to come through cleanly. A pharma stockist wants batch and expiry to survive. An FMCG house wants each of six salesmen locked to his own route.

So "customisable" splits into two very different things. One is configuration: the product bending to your data and your team without anyone writing anything new for you. The other is bespoke work: something genuinely outside the standard product that has to be built or shaped for your case. Most distributors only ever need the first, and it is worth knowing which is which before you buy.

## What the Standard App Already Tailors to Your Business

The core [Takkada app](/blog/what-is-takkada/) is designed to sit on top of the Tally you already run, which means a lot of what feels like customisation is simply the product reading your own setup.

- Your ledgers and parties. Takkada works off your existing Tally masters, so your party names, groups, and outstanding come through as they are, not as a fresh list you re-enter.
- Your invoice formats. GST invoicing on the phone, including e-invoice IRN and e-way bill, follows the formats and tax setup your Tally already uses.
- Your team's shape. Role-based access lets the owner give each user a scope, so a route salesman sees his parties and an accountant gets voucher creation, matching how you already divide work.
- Your add-ons. Capabilities like advanced reports or [PDF purchase import](/blog/import-purchase-from-pdf-tally/) are switched on only where you need them, so the app you run is shaped to your workflow rather than cluttered with everything.

For most distributors, that configuration is the whole of what "customised for my business" needs to mean, and it happens without any special build.

## Where Bespoke Customisation Comes In

Sometimes a business has a requirement that genuinely sits outside the standard product. That is where bespoke customisation enters, and it is available on request rather than as a self-serve feature you turn on yourself.

The reason it works this way is the way Takkada onboards. New distributors are set up hands-on, often with the first sales done end-to-end alongside the partner, so a customisation need surfaces as a real conversation with a real person on our side, not a form you fill in alone. You describe what your business needs, we look at whether the standard product already covers it through configuration, and if it does not, we scope what a tailored fit would involve. There is no claim here of unlimited on-demand customisation, and no shipped self-serve customisation switch. The honest framing is a done-with-you fit: broad configuration first, and bespoke work scoped deliberately when the standard product does not reach.

## How to Scope a Fit Conversation With Us

The useful way to approach this is to come with the specific thing your business does that you are worried about, not a general "can you customise it." A concrete requirement gets a concrete answer.

| What you bring | What we can tell you |
|---|---|
| "My items are shade-wise / batch-wise" | Whether the standard masters and invoicing already carry it |
| "Six salesmen, fixed routes" | How role-based access maps to your route structure |
| "I need a report Tally does not give me" | Whether [Reports+](/blog/mis-reports-for-distributors-tally/) covers it or it needs scoping |
| "Something outside all of the above" | Whether it is configuration or a genuine bespoke fit, and what that involves |

Most of these resolve inside the standard product. The few that do not become a scoped conversation. Either way you get a straight answer about what your money buys and what it does not, which is the point of asking before you commit rather than after.
