## Before the call: 20+ inputs

Every call starts with more than 20 parameters pulled from your books and settings, so the agent never calls blind. They include:

- Your business name, so the party knows who is calling
- The party's name and phone number from the Tally ledger
- The amount due, and the bills behind it
- The language set for that company
- Ageing slab, due dates, collector assigned, previous promises, last reminder sent, and credit terms

Because the inputs come straight from Tally, the amount the agent quotes matches the ledger. Nobody reads out a number from last week's printout.

## During the call

The call itself is short. The agent names the business, says what is due, and asks when it will be paid. It speaks in the party's language.

## After the call: 30+ outputs

This is where most calling falls apart, and where the AI agent does its real work. Each call returns more than 30 parameters. The most important is what the party committed to:

- A promise with a date, such as "Will pay by 22 Sep"
- A dispute, so someone can look at the bill instead of calling again
- A callback request, with when to call back
- An escalation, for parties who need the owner's attention
- The call duration, connection status, partial-payment offer, reason for delay, sentiment, and payment mode mentioned

All of it is logged as a follow-up against the party, on their timeline and on the recovery board, the same place your team's own calls are logged.

## Why the outputs matter more than the call

A call that ends with "theek hai, dekhte hain" and nothing written down is wasted. A call that ends with "promised ₹45,000 by Friday" on the party's record changes what happens next. The next reminder can reference the promise. The owner can see which promises are due this week. A dispute reaches the person who can fix it.

That is the difference between making calls and running collections.
