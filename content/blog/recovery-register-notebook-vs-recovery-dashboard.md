A recovery register in a notebook works well for one person chasing a few dozen parties, and it starts to strain once two or more people share the chasing or the party list grows past what one memory can hold. The notebook is quick, needs no signal, and the owner trusts his own handwriting. What it cannot do is show the same page to the salesman in the field and the accountant in the office at once, or tell you on its own which promised dates passed without payment. A recovery dashboard in Takkada keeps the same entries, who was called, what they said, what they promised and when, against each party, with the name of whoever logged it. After a promised date, it checks the party's receipts in your Tally books and marks the promise kept, part paid or missed, and the missed ones appear in the next morning's brief.

## What the notebook does well

Most distributors have run recovery out of a register for years, and for good reason. Writing "15/10, spoke to party, ₹40,000 by Diwali" takes ten seconds. There is no app to open, no login, and no signal needed in a village. Reading back the last month of entries for one party is a matter of flipping pages, and the owner's own shorthand carries meaning nobody else needs to decode.

For an owner who does all the chasing himself, across 30 or 40 parties, the register is often enough.

## Where the register strains

The strain starts when a second person joins the chasing. The register lives on one desk, so the salesman's promise from the field reaches it only if he remembers to tell the office. Two people end up calling the same party on the same day, each without knowing what the other heard.

The second strain is checking. A promise is only useful if someone looks at it again on the due date and checks whether the money came. With 150 parties and a notebook, that check depends on someone turning to the right page on the right day and then opening Tally to look for the receipt.

The third is continuity. When the person who keeps the register is away, the history goes with the book.

## What the recovery dashboard adds

In Takkada, the same entry goes in from the phone. Tap Mark followed up on the party in Collections or Bills Receivable, pick Call, WhatsApp, In person or Other, pick what happened, and add a promise as an amount with a date. A note holds the shorthand. Every entry carries the name of the person who logged it, and Already tried shows the last five entries before anyone dials. If the phone has no signal, the entry is saved on the device and syncs later.

The checking happens without anyone turning pages. After the promised date, the party's receipts in Tally up to that date decide the status: Promise kept, Part paid or Promise missed. If your Tally data in Takkada has not caught up past the date, the promise waits as Awaiting confirmation. Each morning, the daily brief on the Recovery screen lists Promises due today, Follow-ups due, Missed promises and Top overdue, and the Team tab shows which overdue parties nobody has chased.

## How to decide

Keep the register if one person does all the chasing and the party list fits comfortably in that person's head. Move to the dashboard when the chasing is shared, when promises are made in the field, or when you find yourself asking "who spoke to this party last?" more than once a week. Many owners keep a notebook for their own thinking and let the dashboard hold the team's record.
