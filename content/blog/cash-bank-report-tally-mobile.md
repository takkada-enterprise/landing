## What the Cash and Bank Position Tells You

Of all the numbers a distributor tracks, this is the one with no delay and no dispute. Receivables are money owed, payables are money due, stock is money parked, but the cash and bank position is money you actually have, today, ready to move. It is the hard floor under every payment decision.

An owner about to release a supplier cheque, run salaries, or take a cash discount needs this number first, because a healthy receivables figure does not pay a bill. Only what is in the bank does. Confusing the two, treating money that is coming as money that is here, is how a business with strong sales still bounces a cheque. The cash and bank position is the report that keeps that line clear.

## Where Cash and Bank Live Inside Tally

In Tally Prime, physical cash sits under the Cash-in-hand group and each bank account is a ledger under Bank Accounts. From Gateway of Tally, open Balance Sheet or the Group Summary and drill into these groups, or open a single bank ledger, to read the current balance.

The Group Summary gives you the consolidated cash-and-bank figure and each account within it. Drill into a bank ledger and you see the running balance and the transactions behind it. For a distributor running two or three bank accounts, this is where he sees the spread: which account holds what, so a payment goes out of the account that can carry it. It sits among the [Tally reports an owner reads on mobile](/blog/view-tally-reports-on-mobile/) as the availability anchor.

## Book Balance Versus What Has Actually Cleared

There is a trap in the bank number, and it catches distributors often. Tally shows the book balance: every receipt and payment you have entered. The bank's own balance can differ, because a cheque you issued has not been presented, or a deposit has not cleared, or a charge has not been booked.

The gap between the two is where a payment decision can go wrong. If the book shows comfortable funds but three large cheques are still to clear, the real available balance is lower than it looks. Tally's Bank Reconciliation shows this difference, marking which entries have cleared and which are still in transit, so the owner judges against the balance that has actually settled, not the book figure alone.

## The Reconciliation That Keeps It Honest

A cash and bank position is only trustworthy if it is reconciled. When the book balance and the bank statement drift apart, the position on screen stops being the position in reality, and every payment decision built on it inherits the error.

Reconciling means matching each bank entry to the statement and marking it cleared, which is exactly the work covered in [reconciling a bank statement in Tally from mobile](/blog/how-to-reconcile-bank-statement-tally-mobile/). Where receipts are collected on a link, [auto-reconciliation posts each one back into Tally](/blog/auto-reconciliation-tally/) against the right invoice, so the bank ledger stays current without a nightly matching session and the position you read is one you can trust.
