Yes, you can print barcodes with a normal printer. An office laser printer on A4 sticker sheets prints barcodes that scan reliably, and for a distributor printing stickers for each purchase that arrives, it is often all you need. An inkjet works too, on matte sheets meant for inkjet, though the ink can spread on glossy paper and blur thin bars. A thermal label printer makes sense when you print stickers every day in the hundreds, want one sticker at a time without wasting a sheet, or need labels that come off a roll at the packing table. Whichever printer you use, three things decide whether the barcode scans: bars wide enough for the gun, a blank margin on both sides, and black bars on a white background. Below is a side-by-side for a godown, the print settings that quietly ruin barcodes, and the checks to run on your first sheet.

## Direct thermal or thermal transfer

Thermal printers come in two kinds. Direct thermal printers darken heat-sensitive labels with no ribbon. They are simple, but the labels fade in sunlight and heat, so a carton that sits in a hot godown for months can end up with a grey barcode. Thermal transfer printers melt ink from a ribbon onto the label and last much longer. For stock that turns over in weeks, direct thermal is usually fine. For slow movers and cartons stored outdoors, ask for thermal transfer.

## Print settings that ruin barcodes

- **Fit to page.** The PDF viewer shrinks the sheet a few percent, the barcodes drift off the labels, and the thin bars get thinner. Print at actual size, 100%.
- **Draft or toner-save mode.** Grey bars scan badly. Print in normal quality.
- **Coloured bars.** Red bars are invisible to many red-light scanners. Keep bars black on white.
- **Feeding the sheet the wrong way.** Mark the top edge of the sheet pack, and feed it the same way every time.

## Check the first sheet

Before printing 500 stickers, print one sheet and scan five labels from different corners of it. Then stick one on a carton, the shiny kind if you have it, and scan it from the angle your staff will actually use. If a corner label fails, the sheet is misaligned. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I print barcodes on an inkjet printer?**

A: Yes, on matte sticker sheets made for inkjet. Glossy paper can make the ink spread and blur the thin bars, so test one sheet first.

**Q: Do I need a special barcode printer?**

A: No, you can start without one. A laser printer on A4 sticker sheets works for most distributors. A thermal label printer helps when you print stickers daily in large numbers.

**Q: Why does my printed barcode not scan?**

A: The usual causes are printing with fit to page instead of actual size, bars too thin for the scanner, no blank margin beside the bars, or toner-save mode printing grey bars.

**Q: Do thermal labels fade?**

A: Direct thermal labels can fade in heat and sunlight over months. Thermal transfer labels, printed with a ribbon, last much longer.
