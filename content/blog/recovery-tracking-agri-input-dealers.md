## Credit that runs on the crop calendar

A typical agri-input dealer sells seed, fertiliser and crop protection to 200 to 500 parties, a mix of village retailers and large farmers. Most of it goes out on credit in June and July for kharif, and again in October and November for rabi. Payment comes after harvest, which means 90 to 180 days, and only if the crop and the mandi price both hold.

The promises are made at the counter in sowing season, often with a figure and a rough month. By harvest, the dealer is juggling four hundred of them from memory and a ledger that shows only the balance.

## Recording the promise in sowing season

The Log follow-up sheet takes a promise as an amount and a date together. The date picker reaches a full year ahead, so a promise for after the rabi harvest fits. Add a note for the detail that matters at collection time, such as "pays after cotton sale, son handles money".

Above the form, Already tried shows the last five entries on that party, with who logged each, so the counter staff and the field man see the same history. If a field visit is in a low-signal village, the entry stays on the phone and syncs when the connection returns.

## When harvest money starts coming in

Set the next follow-up date for the week the mandi opens. On that morning, the party appears under Follow-ups due in the daily brief at the top of the Recovery screen. Promises due today lists every party whose promised date is today.

After a promised date passes, the party's receipts in Tally up to that date decide the status: all received reads Promise kept, some reads Part paid, none reads Promise missed. If Takkada's copy of your Tally data has not caught up past that date, the promise shows Awaiting confirmation until it does. In a busy harvest week, that keeps a farmer off the missed list while your books are still catching up.
