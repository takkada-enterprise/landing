## What is a beat in sales on a Guwahati book

Five salesmen. About 120 dealers. Five road names. Station Road is Raju's. GS Road is Manoj's. The other three walk Fancy Bazaar, NH-37 and Beltola. Gupta Stores is a kirana on Station Road, third shutter after the tea stall. When the owner says "Station Road ka beat Raju ka hai," he is naming the list, the person on it, and the shops.

That is what is a beat in sales on this book: a named list, with no calendar on it. The owner [creates beats and assigns a salesman from the phone](/blog/create-beats-and-assign-salesman-mobile/) by typing Station Road, adding the twenty-four shops, and putting Raju on that list. He can leave a beat with no salesman if he is still deciding. The list still exists. The dealers on it are still grouped.

Raju's working set is Station Road because that is the beat he is assigned to. Fancy Bazaar stays Manoj's list. [Restricting salesman access in Tally](/blog/restrict-salesman-access-tally/) is a different job from naming the beat.

## How many dealers fit in one beat

There is no dealer-count field on a beat. How many shops fit is trade practice, the number a salesman can actually stand in front of in a working day, walking or on a scooter, in that market's density.

On this Guwahati book the split is already visible. Station Road holds twenty-four kiranas, a dense stretch Raju covers between morning tea and the last shutter. Fancy Bazaar packs a similar count into a shorter walk. NH-37 is thinner. Fifteen dealers can be a full day when the gaps between shops are measured in kilometres. Beltola sits between those two. About 120 dealers across five lists is twenty-four as a mean, not as a rule the software enforces.

Owners who try to hang forty dealers on one salesman discover the limit in the market, not on a screen. The last eight shops get a wave from the scooter. The beat is still a valid list. The coverage is not. Twenty to thirty is the thumb used in this trade when the shops are close. Rural belts run lower. A wholesale market lane can run higher because the walk is short. None of those numbers are product caps. They are how the book was already walked before anyone named the list in an app.

[Create beats and assign a salesman from the phone](/blog/create-beats-and-assign-salesman-mobile/) does not ask how many dealers you intend to visit. It asks for the name and the parties. The day still belongs to Raju.

## One dealer, one beat, and what moving shows

Gupta Stores belongs to one beat only. The owner cannot park the same party on Station Road and GS Road at once. When he adds Gupta Stores to GS Road, Gupta Stores leaves Station Road, and the app shows what moved. There is no silent copy of the ledger on a second list.

That rule is the whole of the geography. If two salesmen both "have" Gupta Stores, Tuesday produces two visits or none, and the office cannot say whose order it was. One list ends the argument. Manoj picking up Gupta Stores is a move, visible, not a duplicate.

A new kirana on Beltola that has not been placed yet waits off every list. The owner puts it on Beltola when he knows the stretch. Until then it is a Tally party with no beat name, which is a different gap from sitting on two beats.

## Who sits on the beat, and who does not

Each beat has one assigned salesman, or none. Raju on Station Road. Manoj on GS Road. A beat with no salesman is still a beat. The dealers are grouped. Nobody in the field is carrying that list yet.

The owner assigns from Beats. He does not have to finish every list on Monday. Fancy Bazaar can wait a week while he decides between two candidates. The shops stay on Fancy Bazaar. The assignment can be empty.

Dealers on no beat at all are the other pile. They are not on Station Road, not on GS Road, not on the remaining three. They wait until someone places them. That pile is a placement job, covered when you [create beats and assign a salesman](/blog/create-beats-and-assign-salesman-mobile/), not a second kind of beat.

## What the beat list leaves to the salesman

A beat does not tell Raju which shutter to open first. It does not tell him to visit Gupta Stores before 11. It does not hold a sequence, a weekday, or a target. Those things live in Raju's head and in whatever WhatsApp the owner sends at 9:15. "Station Road ka load nikal, Gupta Stores pehle" is an instruction from a person. The beat stays a named list.

The [dispatch load sheet](/blog/dispatch-load-sheet-for-distributors/) is a different object. It totals pending orders. Shops with nothing pending stay on the beat and do not appear on today's load. Raju can still walk them. A [salesman visit tracking app](/blog/salesman-visit-tracking-photo-proof/) stamps the counter when he does. The beat did not require the visit. It only named the shop.

The list is the geography. The walking order is the morning. They stay separate, which is why the same Station Road list still works on a Tuesday when Gupta Stores is closed and Raju starts two shutters down.
