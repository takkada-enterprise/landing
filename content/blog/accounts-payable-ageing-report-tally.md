Paying inside the discount window protects margin, and paying before a supplier chases protects your credit line. ## Why Distributors Skip the Payables Ageing Report

Almost every distributor runs an ageing report on his receivables, because chasing retailers is where the daily pressure sits. Far fewer run one on their payables, and that is the quiet gap. The reasoning feels sound: "I know what I owe, the supplier will remind me." Both halves of that are wrong. You know the lump total, not the due-date schedule inside it, and by the time the supplier reminds you, the cash discount window has usually already closed.

The payables ageing report closes that gap. It takes the [accounts payable position](/blog/accounts-payable-in-tally-for-distributors/) and sorts it by age, so instead of one creditor balance you see a timeline of what falls due when. For a distributor buying ₹4 to ₹5 crore of stock a year, the discipline of paying inside discount windows is often worth more than a chunk of the collection effort he pours onto the receivable side.

## How to Generate It in Tally Prime

The path mirrors the receivables one. From the Gateway of Tally, go to Display More Reports, then Statements of Accounts, then Outstandings, then Payables. That opens the bill-wise payables across all suppliers. Switch to the age-wise view (the F6 Age-Wise toggle in most Tally Prime versions), and set your ageing method and buckets.

Two choices matter. First, age by Due Date rather than Bill Date on the payable side, because what you care about is how far past the agreed payment date each bill has drifted, not how old the purchase is. Second, set buckets that match your terms: 0 to 30, 31 to 60, 61 to 90, over 90 works for most, but if your principals give 45-day terms, shift the boundaries. Bill-wise details must be on for the supplier ledgers, or there are no due dates to age against, the same requirement covered in [bills payable in Tally, explained](/blog/bills-payable-in-tally-explained/).

## Reading the Payable Buckets

Each bucket asks for a different action, and the actions are the opposite of the receivable side.

| Bucket | What it means | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Due this week | Bills approaching their date | Pay inside the discount window; protect margin |
| 0 to 30 overdue | Slipped a little past terms | Clear before it hardens; a call may already be coming |
| 31 to 60 overdue | Supplier is noticing | Pay now or the next order tightens |
| 60+ overdue | Credit line at risk | Owner-level; clear or renegotiate terms openly |

Notice the difference from receivables. On the receivable side, old buckets are the retailer's problem and you chase them. On the payable side, old buckets are your problem and you owe an explanation. The bucket you most want to watch is the "due this week" one rather than the oldest, because that is where the cash discount lives and where it is quietly lost.

## Payables Ageing Versus Receivables Ageing

The two reports are twins, and a distributor benefits from reading them on the same morning. Receivables ageing tells you which cash is coming and how late. Payables ageing tells you which cash must go and how soon. Put side by side, they tell you whether the money arriving covers the money leaving this week.

The mechanics of the receivable version, buckets, the 80/20 rule, the Monday cadence, are in the [ageing report in Tally](/blog/aging-report-tally/), and the mobile-first way to run receivables ageing is covered in [receivables ageing on mobile](/blog/receivables-ageing-on-mobile-tally/). The payable report uses the same buckets and the same drill-down, only the direction of the money and therefore the action is reversed. A distributor who runs both stops being surprised on a Thursday.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is a creditors ageing report?**

A: A creditors ageing report, also called an accounts payable ageing report, buckets your unpaid supplier bills by how long they have been outstanding. It is the mirror of the receivables ageing report, pointed at what you owe instead of what you are owed. It shows which supplier payments are coming due and which are already overdue on your side.

**Q: How do I run a payables ageing report in Tally Prime?**

A: Go to Gateway of Tally, then Display More Reports, then Statements of Accounts, then Outstandings, then Payables, then switch to the age-wise view. Age by Due Date and set buckets that match your credit terms, such as 0 to 30, 31 to 60, 61 to 90, and over 90 days. Bill-wise details must be on for the supplier ledgers.

**Q: Should I age payables by bill date or due date?**

A: Due date, on the payable side. What matters is how far past the agreed payment date each bill has drifted, which tells you whether you are late to the supplier and whether a discount window is closing. Bill date ages by how old the purchase is, which is less useful for scheduling payments.

**Q: How is payables ageing different from receivables ageing?**

A: They use the same buckets and mechanics but opposite direction. Receivables ageing shows money coming in and drives who you chase. Payables ageing shows money going out and drives who you pay and when. A distributor should read both on the same morning to see whether the week's incoming cash covers the outgoing.
