## What the Stock Summary Report Tells a Distributor

Inventory is where a distributor's cash leaves the bank and waits to come back. The stock summary is the report that shows how much cash is waiting, and in what form. Its total is one of the three numbers that make up working capital, sitting alongside receivables and payables, and an owner who does not watch it can be cash-poor while his godown is quietly overfull.

Item by item, the same report answers the most operational question in the business: do we have it. A retailer wants forty cases of a fast mover, and the answer is either yes, ship it, or no, back-order it, and getting that answer wrong either loses a sale or over-promises one. The stock summary holds both the strategic total and the operational detail in one report.

## Where Stock Lives Inside Tally

In Tally Prime, every product is a stock item, and Stock Summary gathers them. From Gateway of Tally, open Stock Summary, and you see each item's closing balance in quantity and value for the current date or period. Switch to the detailed view and each item breaks into opening balance, inward, outward, and closing, so you can see not just what is there but how it got there.

Drill into a single item and you reach its stock item monthly summary and the vouchers behind the movement. Group the view by stock group and you get category-level totals, useful when a distributor carries several product lines. It is the inventory anchor of the [Tally reports an owner reads on mobile](/blog/view-tally-reports-on-mobile/), the one that answers "kitna maal pada hai".

## Two Readers, Two Uses of the Same Report

The stock summary serves two people in the business who read it completely differently.

| Reader | What they look at | The decision |
|---|---|---|
| Owner | Total stock value and category totals | Is too much cash tied up, is a line overstocked, is working capital healthy |
| Salesman | A single item's available quantity | Can I promise this delivery right now |
| Purchase | Items running low against their movement | What to reorder before it goes out of stock |

One report, three decisions. The owner reads it monthly for capital, the salesman reads it hourly for availability, and purchasing reads it against sales for reorder. That is why getting it onto a phone matters, because those readers are rarely at the same desk as Tally.

## Stock in Hand Meets What Is Selling

Stock on its own is only half a decision. A high quantity of an item means overstock if the item is not selling and healthy cover if it is. The stock summary tells you what is there; it cannot tell you whether that is the right amount.

Read against movement, it becomes a reorder and clearance engine. An item with high stock and slow sales is capital to free with a scheme; an item with low stock and fast sales is a reorder before you go dry. That pairing is why the stock summary and the [item-wise sales report](/blog/item-wise-sales-report-tally/) belong side by side, and why the day's [sales report](/blog/daily-sales-report-tally-mobile/) and the stock position together drive the buying rhythm.
