One collection owner per customer is the rule that stops Nagaon at 94 days from belonging to everyone and therefore to no one. Recovery lives inside Collect, available on request. At 8:40 the Guwahati owner sees three overdue parties with no recovery owner, assigns Nagaon and Goalpara to Ramesh before they leave the office, and keeps Tinsukia himself because that counter only takes his call on a Tuesday morning. ## How do I assign each customer's outstanding to one salesman for follow-up?

Pick the party. Put a team member on it as recovery owner. That is the whole assignment. How do I assign each customer's outstanding to one salesman for follow-up is not a Tally voucher problem. A sale already carries a salesman when the order is booked, because the man is standing there. A receipt does not. Money arrives from a current account at 11 PM, and the staffer allocating it is solving which invoice it settles, not whose route it was.

A collection owner per customer is how the chase gets a name anyway. The party master carries the person. Outstanding on that party is then that person's book. The [payment recovery dashboard](/blog/payment-recovery-dashboard-for-distributors/) reads it on Team: Recovered, Outstanding, dealers worked. The [salesman wise collection report](/blog/salesman-wise-collection-accountability/) problem is the same problem. A receipt does not carry a person's name unless the party does.

Do not split one party's outstanding across two salesmen. ₹1,20,000 at Barpeta Chemist is one owner. If two people "both look after him," neither will call on Tuesday, and both will claim the receipt on Friday.

## One collection owner per customer

The working rule is one living person, one outstanding customer. Ramesh owns Nagaon. Suresh owns the Tezpur grocer. The owner of the firm owns Tinsukia because that account only takes his call. Three names, three parties, no overlap.

That rule is what makes Recovered and Outstanding on a person's card mean something. If Nagaon is "the team's," Ramesh's Outstanding is a mood. If Nagaon is Ramesh's, his Outstanding is a rupee figure he can be asked about on Monday, and his Recovered is the money that came in from parties that carry his name.

Dealers worked is the count of those parties, not a visit tally. Speed is median days to collect across the money he actually brought in. Promises kept, missed and open sit on the same card because the follow-up log on those parties is his log. Rank is the board's order, not a prize. None of those numbers work if two people share a chemist and a third person collected the UPI.

Family books try to keep the big accounts unassigned "because Papa handles them." Papa is a recovery owner. Put his name on Tinsukia. Otherwise Tinsukia appears on the nobody-has-chased line, and the Monday conversation starts with a fiction that the account is uncovered.

Bills and Parties do not change when you assign an owner. Ageing as of today is still ageing. The owner field is attribution for the chase, sitting on top of the same ledgers Tally already holds.

## Reassign from the app

Routes move. A salesman quits. A nephew takes a district. The recovery owner has to move with the route, from the app, on the day the change is real, not at month end when the Excel is rebuilt.

Open the party. Reassign the recovery owner. Nagaon that was Ramesh's at 8:40 is Suresh's at 9:05 if Ramesh handed over the route in the doorway. Outstanding on Suresh's card picks up Nagaon's ₹2,40,000. Recovered this month on Ramesh's card still reflects money that came in while the party was his, depending on how you read the period. The honest Monday conversation names the handover: Suresh inherited 94 days he did not build. Do not score him as if he did.

Reassigning from the app is the difference between a live board and a frozen export. An export taken on the 1st still has Ramesh on Nagaon on the 18th, when Ramesh is already at a competitor. The app assignment is the current owner. Attribute outstanding to whoever owns the party today. Keep the history of who billed it if you need it for commission later. The chase has to follow the person who can still walk into the shop.

Do not reassign in a side chat and forget the party. The Team board will keep showing Nagaon on Ramesh, and the [unchased overdue](/blog/overdue-customers-nobody-has-chased/) line will not save you, because the party has an owner even if that owner has left town. The field has to match the route.

## When a salesman leaves

This is the case that breaks books that never named an owner. Ramesh leaves on a Friday. His 40 parties are still in Tally. None of them have a new person. Saturday's brief still looks fine. By the next Friday those 40 have had no call, no WhatsApp, no visit, because the office thought "Ramesh's parties" was a queue someone would pick up.

On the Friday he leaves, sit with the list. Reassign every party from the app before he hands over the phone. Big accounts to the firm owner. The rest split across whoever takes the route. If a route is empty for a week, the owner of the firm is the recovery owner for that week. Empty is a name. "We will see" is how 94-day Nagaon becomes 108-day Nagaon.

The follow-up log stays on the party. Suresh can read that Ramesh called the chemist on Monday and was promised Tuesday. He does not start from zero. Entries are append-only, so he cannot tidy Ramesh's week into a cleaner story. He adds Saturday's visit as himself.

Commission fights belong in a different meeting. The recovery owner is the person who must chase, not automatically the person who earns on the receipt. If those two rules diverge in your firm, write that down. Do not leave it implicit in the app field. The field is for the chase.

## No owner, nobody chased

The Team board names overdue parties nobody has chased. A party with no recovery owner and no log entry this month is how that line gets its names. Nagaon at 94 days, Goalpara at 71, Tinsukia at 88. Three overdue customers, zero people.

Assigning a collection owner per customer is the fix that makes the line shrink. It does not collect the money. It makes one person unable to say "I thought you had it." Next Monday the same cards show whether Recovered moved and whether the nobody-has-chased line got shorter.

The [collection team board](/blog/collection-team-board-recovered-outstanding-speed/) is where that Monday conversation lives. Recovered, Outstanding, Speed, rank. The owner assignment is the input. A board with every party unassigned is a board of zeros and a long nobody-has-chased list. Assign first. Argue Speed later.

Parties at 15 days with no owner are a smaller fire. Start with the overdue names on the brief and on Team. Then walk the rest of the book so new invoices inherit an owner when the salesman books the order, not six weeks later when the bill is already late.

## The salesman at the counter and the owner in Collect

The [Salesman app for Tally](/salesman-app-tally) is the field half. The rep at the counter sees his own parties, takes the order, and can see what they owe. Recovery's owner field is the office half: the name the Monday board uses when that rep is not in the room.

Those two have to agree. If the salesman app shows Nagaon as Ramesh's beat and Recovery still has no owner, the office will not chase and Ramesh will think the office is chasing. Put the same person in both places. Reassign both when the route moves.

Tally stays the neighbour. You are not creating a second party master. You are naming, on the party Tally already has, who must pick up the phone. Outstanding receivables remain the book's numbers. The recovery owner is the person who will be asked about those numbers on Monday.

On that Tuesday the ageing report still shows ₹18 lakh overdue. With owners assigned, it also shows whose ₹18 lakh. That is the only change that matters before the first call.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How do I assign each customer's outstanding to one salesman for follow-up?**

A: Put a recovery owner, a team member, on that party from the app. One person per outstanding customer. Reassign from the app when the route changes or a salesman leaves. Do not leave big accounts unnamed because the proprietor handles them. The proprietor is a recovery owner too.

**Q: What is a collection owner per customer?**

A: A collection owner per customer is the team member whose name sits on that party for follow-up. Outstanding, recovered, promises, and dealers worked on the Team board all read from that name. A receipt still lands in Tally against the bill. The owner field is how the chase, not the voucher, gets a person.

**Q: Can two salesmen share one overdue customer?**

A: Do not. Split routes by party, not by rupees on the same ledger. If two people both look after a chemist, neither calls on Tuesday and both claim Friday's receipt. One party, one recovery owner.

**Q: What happens when a salesman leaves?**

A: Reassign every one of his parties from the app before he hands over the phone. The follow-up log stays on the party for the next person to read. Parties left unnamed become the overdue customers nobody has chased.

**Q: Does assigning an owner change Tally outstanding?**

A: No. Ageing and bills stay as Tally has them. The recovery owner is attribution for the chase. Tally remains the book of record.

**Q: Where do I see parties with no owner?**

A: On the Team board, in the line that names overdue parties nobody has chased, and on the daily brief when those names are in the morning queue. Assign them from the app before the meeting ends.
