## How do I assign dealers to a salesman's beat?

From Beats, on the phone. The owner types a name the market already uses, Station Road, adds the dealers who sit on that stretch, ticks the days the beat runs and picks who runs it on each of those days. That is the whole of beat assignment. Raju's name goes on Station Road. Manoj's name goes on GS Road. Fancy Bazaar, NH-37 and Beltola get the other three.

The [Salesman app for Tally](/salesman-app-tally) is where this list lives for the field. [What a beat is in distribution sales](/blog/what-is-a-beat-in-distribution-sales/) is the named list itself. This article is the Monday job of building it from the phone while Raju is still on the road.

He WhatsApps at 4:40. "Fancy Bazaar mein naya kirana hai, Gupta ke baad wali gali." The owner opens Beats, finds Fancy Bazaar, adds the party, and the shop is on a list before the van comes back. That is why the how-to stays on the phone even though Beats also run on the web.

## Open Beats on the phone

The screen is called Beats. It is a name plus a dealer list, the weekdays it runs, and a salesman for each of those days or none. There is no date field, no walking order, and no visit target. The owner is naming Station Road. The walking order is still Raju's to decide.

He starts empty. About 120 Tally parties sit in the books and none of them are on a beat yet. He types Station Road, searches Gupta Stores, adds it, then the next kirana, until twenty-four shops sit on that list. He ticks the days Station Road runs and picks Raju for them. He repeats for GS Road with Manoj, then Fancy Bazaar, NH-37 and Beltola.

A beat can exist with no salesman. Fancy Bazaar can wait while he decides between two candidates. The shops stay grouped. Nobody in the field is carrying that list until a name is assigned.

Member permissions set view and create rights per register, restrict which ledgers (parties) a member sees, and restrict which voucher types he can use. Raju's working set becomes Station Road. That is the same discipline as [restricting salesman access in Tally](/blog/restrict-salesman-access-tally/), applied after the beat exists. The assignment is the list. The permission is who may open the parties on it.

## Beat assignment salesman on Station Road

Beat assignment salesman work on this book is a person on a named list. Raju on Station Road. Gupta Stores among the twenty-four. When the owner puts Raju on Station Road for its days, the field job has a boundary. On those days Manoj does not walk those shutters, and Raju does not walk GS Road unless he is picked for it.

The owner can change the person later. If Raju is out for a week, Station Road can move to a fill-in, or sit with none until he is back. The dealer list does not have to be rebuilt. The assignment is the salesman picked for each day on that beat.

[Beat-wise sales management](/blog/beat-wise-sales-management-tally-distributors/) is this list plus today's load and the order into Tally. Beat assignment is only the first of those. Until Raju is on Station Road, Today's load has no salesman to open it. The godown can still see a [dispatch load sheet](/blog/dispatch-load-sheet-for-distributors/) for the beat. The field cannot.

An order Raju books after he is assigned lands in Tally as a Sales Order, which is the point of [salesman order taking without re-entry](/blog/salesman-order-to-tally-without-reentry/). The beat did not create the voucher. It named the party he was standing in front of.

## Beats by weekday

Station Road does not need Raju every day. In Beats, under Days, the owner ticks the weekdays a route runs, and the beat runs only on the ticked days. For each ticked day he picks who runs it, so the app asks "Who runs it on Tuesday?" and he can pick Manoj for Tuesday while Raju keeps Monday and Thursday. A beat with no days ticked runs every day, so beats made before weekdays existed carry on unchanged. On the web, the Week view shows every salesman down the side and Monday to Sunday across, with today marked.

| Beat | Days ticked | Who runs it |
|---|---|---|
| Station Road | Mon, Tue, Thu | Raju on Mon and Thu, Manoj on Tue |
| GS Road | Mon to Sat | Manoj |
| Fancy Bazaar | none ticked | Runs every day |

A salesman scheduled on any day of a beat can see that beat's shops. For the same idea in Hinglish, read [beat plan kya hota hai](/blog/beat-plan-kya-hota-hai/).

## The one-beat rule, and the move the app shows

Gupta Stores belongs to one beat only. The owner cannot add it to GS Road and leave a copy on Station Road. When he adds Gupta Stores to GS Road, Gupta Stores leaves Station Road, and the app shows what moved. The move is the confirmation. There is no silent duplicate.

That is the behaviour to watch for on Monday afternoon, when two brothers both claim Gupta Stores. The owner picks GS Road. Station Road loses the party. Raju sees it leave. Manoj sees it arrive. The app showing what moved is how both of them know the argument ended.

If the owner added Gupta Stores to GS Road expecting a shared call, he undoes the move by putting it back on Station Road. Same rule, other direction. Each party sits on one list.

A [field order collection app for Tally](/blog/field-order-collection-app-tally/) still writes the order against the Tally party. The beat only decides whose list that party sits on when the salesman opens his phone.

## Unassigned: dealers on no beat

Unassigned lists dealers on no beat. The new kirana Raju messaged about, before the owner added it to Fancy Bazaar, sits here. So does a party the office created in Tally on Friday and has not placed yet. About 120 dealers on five roads means the leftovers are the ones still in this view.

Only full-access members see Unassigned. Raju does not. Manoj does not. The owner, and anyone else with full access, can open it and place a shop. That is the point of keeping the pile off the field phones. A salesman who sees every unplaced party starts treating them as his.

Placement is the same Beats screen. Pick the dealer from Unassigned, put it on Beltola, assign whoever walks Beltola, or leave Beltola with its existing salesman. The party leaves Unassigned because it now belongs to a beat.

Unassigned is not a sixth beat. It has no salesman field. It is the list of parties that have not been grouped yet.

## Beats also work on the web

Beats also work on the web, on the office desktop, the same lists. The owner can build all five roads on Monday morning at the desk if he wants. This how-to stays on the phone because the useful moment is 4:40, when Raju is in Fancy Bazaar and a shutter has to go on a list before evening.

Visit capture waits for the phone. Beats do not. The owner can finish Station Road on the web, then add one kirana from his pocket at a wedding. Both edits land on the same named list. Raju opens Station Road the next morning and Gupta Stores is either on it or it has moved, and the app already showed what moved when the edit happened.

Five salesmen, about 120 dealers, five road names. The screen is called Beats. Type the name, add the shops, tick the days and pick who runs each one. That is beat assignment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How do I assign dealers to a salesman's beat?**

A: Open Beats on the phone. Type the beat name, add the dealers, tick the days it runs and pick a salesman for each day, or leave the list with none. Station Road gets Raju and twenty-four shops. GS Road gets Manoj. The screen is called Beats.

**Q: What does beat assignment salesman work look like when a dealer already sits on another beat?**

A: A dealer belongs to one beat only. Adding Gupta Stores to GS Road moves it off Station Road, and the app shows what moved. There is no silent copy of the same party on a second list.

**Q: Can the same beat have a different salesman on different days?**

A: Yes. Tick the days the beat runs and pick who runs it on each one, so Station Road can go to Raju on Monday and Thursday and to Manoj on Tuesday. A beat with no days ticked runs every day.

**Q: Who can see dealers that are on no beat?**

A: Unassigned lists dealers on no beat, and only full-access members see that view. Raju does not. The owner places those parties onto Station Road or Beltola from the same Beats screen.

**Q: Can I build beats on the web instead of the phone?**

A: Yes. Beats also work on the web. The lists are the same. This how-to stays on the phone because the owner often has to add a shop while the salesman is still in the market.

**Q: Does assigning a salesman put a date or a walking order on the beat?**

A: No. A beat is a name plus a dealer list, the weekdays it runs and a salesman for each of those days. It carries no date, sequence, or visit target, and a beat with no days ticked runs every day. Raju still decides which shutter he stands in front of first.

**Q: Can a beat exist with no salesman on it?**

A: Yes. A beat can have a salesman for each day it runs, or none. Fancy Bazaar can sit as a named list while the owner decides who walks it. The dealers stay grouped. Unassigned is the other pile, parties on no beat at all.
