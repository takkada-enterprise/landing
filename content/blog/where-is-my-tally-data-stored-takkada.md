## Where Your Tally Data Actually Sits

The plainest way to answer "where is my data" is to split it in two, because your Tally data lives in two places once a phone is involved, and they are not equal.

The first place is your office machine. Your Tally Prime installation and the company file sit there, on hardware you own, and that stays the master copy of your books. Every voucher and every year of history is physically in your office. This is the same point the guide on [connecting an app to Tally safely](/blog/is-it-safe-to-connect-app-to-tally/) makes: an app is a window onto your books, not a new home for them.

The second place is the synced slice. So a phone outside your office can show live outstanding and ledgers, a working copy of the operational data, party balances, registers, stock, recent vouchers, is synced to secure storage held in an India region. That is the copy your salesman sees on the road. Your full history and the master file stay in the office. If you want that synced layer on your own infrastructure instead, the [own-server option](/blog/tally-data-on-your-own-server/) covers how that is arranged.

## How the Data Is Protected as It Moves

Data is most exposed when it travels: from your office to storage, and from storage to a phone on a mobile network. That movement is protected by encryption in transit, which means the data is scrambled while it moves so it cannot be read by anyone intercepting the connection. It arrives readable only at the two ends that are meant to have it, your storage and your authorised app.

Encryption in transit is the ordinary, expected protection for any service that carries business data over the internet, and the right thing to check is that a vendor uses it rather than sending your numbers in the clear. The point for a distributor is simple: your party balances and margins are not exposed on the wire between your office and your phone.

## Who on the Vendor Side Can See Your Data

This is the question most owners actually mean when they ask "who can see it." The honest answer has to name the vendor side, not just your own team.

Access on our side is controlled and limited. Your synced data is not open for anyone to browse. Reaching it requires authorisation, and it is restricted to a small set of support staff, for a specific reason, such as helping you fix a sync issue you have raised. It is not a data set that gets passed around or looked at casually. A serious vendor should be able to say this in plain words, and if one cannot explain who on their side can see your data and why, that hesitation is itself the answer. The checklist in the [is it safe to connect an app to Tally](/blog/is-it-safe-to-connect-app-to-tally/) guide is exactly the set of questions worth putting to any vendor, Takkada included.

## Who on Your Own Team Can See What

The other half of visibility is inside your own business, and here you are the one in control. Role-based access lets the owner assign each team member a role and a scope, so people see only what their job needs.

| Team member | What they see |
|---|---|
| Owner | Full view: all parties, outstanding, margins, reports |
| Accountant / operator | Ledgers and voucher creation, as their role allows |
| Route salesman | Only the parties on his route, and the actions you permit |

This is the same discipline you would apply to a paper ledger, where not everyone gets every page. A new salesman on a Dibrugarh route gets his parties and the ability to raise an invoice, and he does not get the whole company's margins. You set this at the start, and it decides who among your own people sees what.

## What You Keep Control Of

Putting your books on a phone does not mean handing over the keys for good. Three controls stay with you, and together they are the substance of whether the arrangement is safe.

Your Tally stays the master copy. Every audit and year-end close still runs off the office machine, and if the app and Tally ever disagree, Tally is the record. The app also does not quietly edit your existing books; that boundary is spelled out in [does Takkada change my Tally data](/blog/does-takkada-change-my-tally/). You decide who on your team sees what, through the roles above. And you can ask, in plain terms, where the data sits, who can reach it, and what happens to your synced copy when you stop. A vendor who answers those without flinching is one you can judge on facts instead of faith.
