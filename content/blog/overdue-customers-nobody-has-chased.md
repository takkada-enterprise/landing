## How do I find overdue customers that no one on my team has followed up with?

How do I find overdue customers that no one on my team has followed up with is not answered by sorting the ageing report by days. That report will happily list 60 parties past 60 days, including the chemist you called last night and the grocer who promised Friday. You need the cut that ageing cannot make: overdue, and untouched. That cut is the Team line that names overdue parties nobody has chased.

Open Collect, open Recovery, open Team. The line is on that board, next to the member cards and the company roll-up. It is also echoed on the [daily collection brief](/blog/daily-collection-brief-for-distributor-owners/) when those names belong in the morning queue. The [payment recovery dashboard](/blog/payment-recovery-dashboard-for-distributors/) is those two surfaces working together. Parties still shows ageing as of today. The unchased line shows which of those aged parties has had zero chase.

Without it, the owner exports [receivables ageing on mobile](/blog/receivables-ageing-on-mobile-tally/), highlights the 90-plus column, and still does not know whether Ramesh already visited Nagaon. The visit lived in a WhatsApp thread. The export does not read WhatsApp. The line reads the recovery owner and the follow-up log, which is why those two fields have to exist before the line can tell the truth.

## Why unchased overdue customers hide inside a total

A book of 180 parties will always have a total overdue figure. ₹18 lakh is a temperature. It is not a task. Inside that ₹18 lakh sit three kinds of party that need three kinds of Tuesday.

The first kind has a recovery owner and a live promise. Barpeta Chemist, ₹45,000 dated today. He is overdue, and he is being chased. He must not appear as unchased. Putting him on the nobody-has-chased line would send a second person to repeat Monday's call.

The second kind has an owner who has gone quiet. Tezpur is Suresh's, last log entry three weeks ago, still 71 days. That is a coaching problem with Suresh, visible on his card as Outstanding and slow Speed, not as an unowned name.

The third kind has no owner and no log. Nagaon, Goalpara, Tinsukia. Those are unchased overdue customers. They hide because the total does not split itself. The office looks at ₹18 lakh, feels the weight, and starts with whoever is geographically convenient. The three untouched names wait. By next month they are still in the total, a little older, still nobody's.

Family firms produce a fourth hiding place: "Papa handles the big ones." Papa is not written on the party. Tinsukia at 88 days looks unchased to the board even though Papa thinks he owns it. Write Papa as the recovery owner, or Tinsukia will keep appearing on the line, and the meeting will keep arguing with the software instead of calling the shop.

## The nobody-has-chased line on Team

Team is cards with sort chips: Recovered, Outstanding, Speed as median days to collect, dealers worked, promises kept missed and open, rank, a company roll-up. The unchased line sits with that roll-up. It is a list of names, not a fourth rupee total.

On Tuesday it might read three parties. Nagaon retailer, 94 days. Goalpara grocer, 71 days. Tinsukia party, 88 days. Amounts on Bills if you open them. The line's job is the names. Once you have the names, you have a meeting.

The [collection team board](/blog/collection-team-board-recovered-outstanding-speed/) article is the rest of those cards. This page is only the line owners underrate. A board that is otherwise healthy, Recovered up, Speed reasonable, can still be leaking through three untouched 90-day accounts. Those three are often the ones that become a lawyer problem while the team is celebrating a good collection week on current billing.

Read the line every Monday before you praise Recovered. If it grew, the praise is incomplete. If it shrank, someone actually did the ugly work. That is the only scoreboard that counts for this job.

## No recovery owner, no log entry

Two missing fields, together, are the definition you can operate.

No recovery owner means the party is not on anyone's card as Outstanding they must answer for. Ramesh can drive past Nagaon every Thursday and still honestly say it is not his. The [collection owner per customer](/blog/assign-recovery-owner-per-customer/) field is what makes it his.

No follow-up log entry, at least not in the period you are reading, means nobody recorded a call, WhatsApp, visit, or other, with an outcome and a next-action date. The chase that happened only in someone's head did not happen for this line. Append-only entries are how a real chase knocks a party off the unchased list: you called, you logged, next action is dated. Even a no-answer on Saturday is a chase. Silence is not.

A party can have an owner and still be neglected. That shows on the owner's card, not on the unchased line. Keep those two signals separate or you will reassign parties that already have a lazy owner, and the lazy owner will look cleaner than he is.

Bills and Parties do not care about either field. Ageing as of today is still ageing. The unchased line is a filter on top of that ageing, using owner and log. Tally remains the book of record. You are not creating a second overdue number. You are naming the overdue that has had no person.

## The Tuesday assignment

The meeting is short when the line is short. Three names. Ramesh takes Nagaon and Goalpara because they sit on his route. The proprietor takes Tinsukia because that counter only picks up for him. Reassign the recovery owner in the app before anyone leaves the room. Set a next-action date on each, even if today's outcome is only that you assigned them. Tomorrow's brief can then name the follow-up.

Do not "discuss Nagaon" and walk out with the field still blank. The line will reprint the same three names next Monday, and you will have had a meeting about a screenshot. The app assignment is the minutes.

If the line is twenty names, do not pretend you will clear it today. Take five. Put owners on those five. Date the next action. Leave the other fifteen on the line in the open. A line that always shows twenty because you never assign is a line you have decided to ignore. Shrink it in batches. The honesty is the remaining names still being visible.

A no-answer after assignment is still progress. Log it. The party is no longer unchased. They may still be unpaid. Unchased and unpaid are different problems. This page is only the first.

## What changes by next Monday

Next Monday the same cards show whether Recovered moved, whether promises were kept, and whether the nobody-has-chased line shrank. That last one is the test of Tuesday's assignment.

If Nagaon now has Ramesh as owner, a Saturday no-answer on the log, and a next-action of Wednesday, Nagaon is off the unchased line even if ₹2,40,000 is still open. Good. The money is a different conversation, on Ramesh's Outstanding and Speed.

If Tinsukia is still on the line, the proprietor did not write himself as owner, or he did and nobody logged the call he claims he made. Either way the line is correct. The meeting is about the field, not about memory.

Over a month the shape you want is a short unchased line and a Team board whose Outstanding sits on named people. [Days sales outstanding](/blog/days-sales-outstanding-distributor-india/) may still be 58. That company number can hide a clean chase or a dirty one. The unchased line tells you which. A falling DSO with a long unchased list means you collected from the easy parties and left the silent ones to rot. A steady DSO with a short unchased list means the ugly names are at least being asked.

Tally keeps every bill through that month. Unchased overdue customers are a people gap, not a ledger gap. Fill the people gap from the app. Then collect.

**Q: How do I find overdue customers that no one on my team has followed up with?**

A: Open the Team board in Recovery and read the line that names overdue parties nobody has chased. Those are parties past terms with no recovery owner and no follow-up on the log. The ageing total will not split them out for you.

**Q: What are unchased overdue customers?**

A: Unchased overdue customers are overdue parties nobody has chased: no recovery owner, no dated follow-up, still past terms. They hide inside the book total. The Team line names them so Monday can assign them.

**Q: Does a WhatsApp reminder count as a chase for this line?**

A: The line reads recovery owner and the follow-up log, not the company-wide reminder cadence. A scheduled reminder can still leave. The party stays unchased until a person owns it and a log entry exists. Cadence is not a substitute for the assignment.

**Q: What should I do with the names on the line?**

A: Assign a recovery owner from the app before the meeting ends, and log a next-action date. Split the names across people who can actually walk into those shops. Do not leave the field blank after discussing it.

**Q: Can a party with an owner still appear as unchased?**

A: The unchased line is for parties nobody has chased, typically with no owner and no log. A party with a lazy owner shows on that owner's Outstanding and Speed instead. Keep those two signals separate so you coach the right person.

**Q: Will assigning an owner collect the money?**

A: No. Assigning an owner makes one person unable to say they thought someone else had it. The bills stay in Tally until a receipt allocates. The line shrinking means the people gap closed. The rupee gap is the rest of the week.
