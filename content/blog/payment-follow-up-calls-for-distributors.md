The reminder went out at nine in the morning. Two blue ticks by eleven. Two days later, the bill is still open.

Every distributor knows this moment. WhatsApp reminders work for the parties who were going to pay anyway. For the rest, the next step is a phone call, and that is where collections quietly stall.

## Why the follow-up call doesn't happen

Nobody in the office wants to make it. The accountant is busy with entries. The salesman doesn't want to spoil the relationship with a shop he visits every week. The owner means to do it after lunch, then after dispatch, then tomorrow.

When the call does happen, three things go wrong:

1. **It happens late.** A party who gets a call on day 3 behaves differently from one who gets it on day 30.
2. **It happens unevenly.** The big parties get called. The forty small ones with ₹8,000 each never do, and together they are a large number.
3. **Nothing gets written down.** "Sahab, Friday tak kar denge" is said on the phone, and nobody records the Friday. Next week someone calls again and hears the same promise.

## What a follow-up call actually needs to do

A good collection call is short and boring. It says who is calling, what is due, and asks when it will be paid. Then it writes down the answer, whether that is a date, a dispute, or "call me back on Monday".

That is exactly the kind of task a machine can do every day without getting tired or embarrassed.
