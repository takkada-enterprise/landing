What is ladder discount? It is a set of discounts applied one after the other on the same price, with each step taken off what is left after the step before. The trade also calls it a chain, series or compound discount, and in the market it is simply "discount par discount". Because every step works on a smaller base, the steps never add up. A ladder of 10% + 5% + 2% on ₹100 goes to ₹90.00, then ₹85.50, then ₹83.79, so the real discount is 16.21%, not 17%. The formula is net price = list price × (1 − d1) × (1 − d2) × (1 − d3), and the single equivalent discount is 100% minus that product. Distributors meet ladders every day: a company scheme, then the trade discount, then a cash discount for paying on time. The order of the steps does not change the answer. The rounding does, and that is where most bill disputes start.

## Why 10 + 5 + 2 is not 17

The second discount is a percentage of the price after the first, so it is worth less than it looks. On ₹100, the 10% step takes ₹10.00. The 5% step is worked on ₹90, so it takes ₹4.50, and the 2% step is worked on ₹85.50, so it takes ₹1.71. Together that is ₹16.21 off.

This is the call every distributor has taken: "bhai, 17% bola tha, bill mein 16 kaise aaya?" Nobody cheated. The retailer added, the bill multiplied.

## The formula, and a shortcut for two steps

Write each discount as a fraction left over, multiply them, and subtract from one.

- Net price = list price × (1 − d1) × (1 − d2) × (1 − d3)
- Equivalent discount = 100% − (net price ÷ list price)

For two steps there is a mental shortcut: a + b − (a × b ÷ 100). So 20 + 10 is 30 − 2, which is 28%.

| Ladder | Added up | Real discount |
|---|---|---|
| 10% + 5% | 15% | 14.50% |
| 20% + 10% | 30% | 28.00% |
| 5% + 5% + 5% | 15% | 14.26% |
| 10% + 5% + 2% | 17% | 16.21% |
| 15% + 5% + 2% | 22% | 20.87% |
| 25% + 10% + 5% | 40% | 35.88% |

Real discounts are rounded half up to two decimals.

## A ₹25,000 line, worked

Take a line worth ₹25,000 at list rate, before GST, with a ladder of 12% scheme + 5% trade + 1.5% cash.

- Step 1: ₹25,000 less 12% is ₹22,000.00
- Step 2: ₹22,000 less 5% is ₹20,900.00
- Step 3: ₹20,900 less 1.5% is ₹20,586.50

The exact equivalent is 0.88 × 0.95 × 0.985, which leaves 82.346%, so the discount is 17.654%. Tally's discount column holds two decimals, so the line goes in at 17.65%. That makes the discount ₹4,412.50 and the taxable value ₹20,587.50, one rupee above the step-by-step figure. At 12% GST, CGST and SGST are ₹1,235.25 each, and the line total is ₹23,058.00.

That rupee is the whole lesson. Pick the rounded figure once, and make the bill your customer checks and the voucher in Tally use the same one. A line where the office compounds on a calculator and the bill rounds differently will not reconcile, and the payment waits until somebody explains why. ## Ladder discount vs slab discount

Some companies use "ladder scheme" for a volume slab: buy 10 cases and get 2%, buy 25 and get 4%. That is a staircase on quantity, and only one rate applies to the bill. A ladder discount in the billing sense is several steps stacked on the same line. Read the scheme letter for which one the company means before you set up the rate.

## Where GST comes in

A discount printed on the invoice at the time of supply comes off before GST, and tax is charged on what is left (Section 15(3)(a) of the CGST Act). A cash discount given later, when the retailer pays, is a post-supply discount and normally travels on a credit note, with its own conditions. [Quantity discount vs cash discount under GST](/blog/quantity-discount-vs-cash-discount-gst) covers that split in full.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is ladder discount the same as compound discount?**

A: Yes. Ladder, chain, series and compound discount all describe discounts taken one after another on a reducing price. "Discount par discount" is the same thing in market language.

**Q: Does the order of the steps change the total?**

A: No. Multiplication gives the same result in any order, so 5% + 10% + 2% and 10% + 5% + 2% both come to 16.21%.

**Q: How do I turn a ladder into one discount for Tally?**

A: Multiply the fractions left after each step, subtract the result from one, and round to two decimals. For 10% + 5% + 2%, that is 1 − (0.90 × 0.95 × 0.98), which is 16.21%.

**Q: Is a ladder discount charged before or after GST?**

A: Before, when it is printed on the invoice at the time of sale. GST is worked on the value left after every step.
