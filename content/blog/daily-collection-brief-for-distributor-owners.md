## What should I check every morning to collect payments faster?

The printed outstanding list is the wrong first page. It matches Tally. It also puts the chemist who promised ₹45,000 yesterday at row 40, under parties who have been at 90 days since April. What you should check every morning to collect payments faster is a short card that names only what is due from yesterday's chase: promises due today, follow-ups due today, promises proven missed, receipts awaiting confirmation, and the top overdue parties. When none of those apply, the card says All clear.

That card is the daily collection brief. It sits in Recovery, inside Collect, on the same [payment recovery dashboard](/blog/payment-recovery-dashboard-for-distributors/) as Parties, Bills, Habits and Team. [Outstanding receivables on mobile](/outstanding-receivables-on-mobile) still answers the book total. The brief answers the narrower morning question: who needs a person before the first retailer call.

Without it, the morning is a WhatsApp thread and a feeling. Ramesh will start with the party on his way. The owner will start with whoever called yesterday. The accountant will start with whoever shouted. Three different first calls, none of them necessarily the ₹45,000 that was dated for today.

## What the daily collection brief names

Six possible contents, always the same vocabulary.

**Promises due today.** A dated promise whose promised date is today. Barpeta Chemist, ₹45,000, logged Monday 7 PM after a call. That line comes from [promise to pay tracking](/blog/promise-to-pay-tracking-distributors/) on the follow-up log. The brief does not invent the date. It reads the date you stored.

**Follow-ups due today.** A next-action date you set after a no-answer, a dispute, or a visit that needed a check. The Dibrugarh kirana with no answer on Saturday sits here on Monday. The Tezpur grocer marked disputed on a rate difference sits here on the day you said you would pull the invoice.

**Promises proven missed.** A promise whose date has passed, with no receipt in Tally for that party, after allowance for sync lag. The Nalbari party who said Friday, and whose ledger still shows no receipt on Monday, is this line. Proven missed, not a midnight flag.

**Receipts awaiting confirmation.** Money may have landed. Tally may not have it yet. The brief names that gap so you do not call a party whose UPI is already in the bank.

**Top overdue parties.** The heavy end of the book, so the card is not only about yesterday's promises. A Nagaon retailer at 94 days still belongs in the morning even if nobody dated a promise against him.

**All clear.** None of the above. The card says so, in those words, instead of showing an empty screen the owner has to interpret.

Those six are the whole brief. There is no seventh line for "motivational total collected this month." Recovered and Outstanding live on the Team board. The brief is the day's queue.

## The owner opens the card

The daily brief is a card in the app. The owner opens Collect, opens Recovery, and reads it. On a Tuesday in August the Guwahati FMCG book might look like this.

One promise due today: Barpeta Chemist, ₹45,000, logged yesterday after a call, next action today. Two follow-ups due: a Dibrugarh kirana that had no answer on Saturday, and a Tezpur grocer marked disputed on a rate difference. One promise proven missed: a Nalbari party who said Friday. Three overdue parties with no recovery owner.

That is the morning. Six lines, maybe eight. The owner can finish the card before the tea is cold. He then decides who takes which line. The chemist he will call himself at 9:10. Ramesh takes Dibrugarh because it is on the route. The accountant pulls the Tezpur invoice before 10. The three unowned overdue names go to whoever is standing in the office, with a recovery owner assigned before they leave.

The card does not place the calls. It does not message the chemist. It names the work. The person still has to do the work. That is the honest half of a morning system. A list you open is a list you can act on. A list you wait to receive is a list you will ignore on the day the phone is busy.

Habits and Bills are one tap away if a line needs context. The chemist at ₹45,000 is three invoices on Bills. Habits will show whether 62 days is normal for him. The brief does not duplicate those tabs. It points at the party. You open the party when the line is yours.

## Sharing the brief by hand

If the accountant or a salesman should see the same six lines, the owner shares the brief as text through the phone's share sheet. WhatsApp, email, anywhere the phone can send text. The share is manual.

That sentence is load-bearing. The brief is not pushed out of the phone on a schedule. The owner decides that today's card is worth sending, opens the share sheet, and picks a chat. Some Tuesdays he sends it to the office group at 8:45. Some Tuesdays he keeps it, because the six lines are all his. The product does not choose for him.

The shared object is text. It can sit in the same WhatsApp thread as the rest of the day's operations. It can go to email if the accountant lives in the inbox. It can go to a salesman who is already on the highway. Because it is text, it does not require the recipient to open Recovery first. Because the share is manual, a quiet All clear Tuesday does not have to land in twenty chats.

Do not confuse this share with reminder cadence. Cadence is company-wide WhatsApp to retailers, on a preset, about invoices and due dates. The brief share is owner to team, about today's chase list. Different audience, different button, different day.

## Receipts awaiting confirmation

This is the line that stops a false chase. A retailer pays at 11:40 PM. The bank has it. Tally does not, until the receipt is posted. If the brief treated every open bill as a person who has not paid, the owner would call the chemist at 8:50 and hear "bhai, last night bhej diya." That call costs more than the money. It trains the chemist to dread the morning number.

Receipts awaiting confirmation names the gap instead. The owner sees that something is in flight. He waits, or he checks the bank, or he asks the accountant. He does not start with a recovery speech. On a book that takes UPI late at night, this line will appear often. That is a healthy book, not a broken one.

The same lag sits under proven missed. A promise is marked missed only after the promised date has passed and no receipt has landed in Tally for that party, with allowance for sync lag. The brief's waiting-confirmation line is how that allowance shows up before the miss is proven. Two sides of one delay.

[Outstanding receivables on mobile](/outstanding-receivables-on-mobile) will still show the bill as open until the receipt allocates. The brief is allowed to be more careful than the outstanding total for a few hours in the morning. That care is the difference between a collection system and a nag.

## All clear, and the quiet Tuesday

All clear is a real state. No promises due today, no follow-ups due today, no proven misses, no receipts waiting, and the top overdue list is empty enough that the card says so. The owner still has an ageing book. He does not have a dated chase for this morning.

On that Tuesday he can walk the [unchased overdue customers](/blog/overdue-customers-nobody-has-chased/) on the Team board, or he can spend the hour on billing. He should not invent work to make the card look busy. All clear means yesterday's promises were kept or moved, and today's queue was not pre-loaded. That is a good Tuesday.

Most books will not see All clear in the week after you switch Recovery on. The first mornings surface dates that were living in WhatsApp. After a fortnight the card shrinks, because next-action dates are real and receipts are catching up. If the card stays long forever, the next-action dates are being set too densely, or promises are being logged without amounts. Tighten the log. Do not blame the brief.

Tally keeps the ledgers either way. The daily collection brief only names the slice of those ledgers that a person should touch before lunch.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What should I check every morning to collect payments faster?**

A: Open the daily brief card and read promises due today, follow-ups due today, promises proven missed, receipts awaiting confirmation, and the top overdue parties, or All clear. That card is the morning queue. The full outstanding list is still there for later. The first calls should come from the brief.

**Q: What is a daily collection brief?**

A: A daily collection brief is a card in the Recovery view inside Collect that names the chase due today. It lists promises due today, follow-ups due today, promises proven missed, receipts awaiting confirmation, top overdue parties, or All clear. The owner opens it.

**Q: How do I share the daily collection brief with my accountant?**

A: Open the card and share it as text through the phone's share sheet, to WhatsApp, email, or anywhere the phone can send text. The share is manual. The brief is not sent out on its own.

**Q: Does the brief include every overdue party?**

A: No. It names top overdue parties plus the dated chase: promises, follow-ups, proven misses, and receipts awaiting confirmation. The rest of the book sits on Parties and Bills. The brief is the short list for before lunch, not a reprint of the ageing report.

**Q: What does receipts awaiting confirmation mean?**

A: It means money may have landed and Tally may not have the receipt yet. Treat that line as a pause, not as a chase. A UPI credit late at night that posts in the morning should not produce an 8:50 recovery call.

**Q: What should I do when the card says All clear?**

A: Treat it as a real state. There is no dated chase for this morning. Use the hour on the Team board's overdue parties nobody has chased, or on billing. Do not invent follow-ups to fill the card.
