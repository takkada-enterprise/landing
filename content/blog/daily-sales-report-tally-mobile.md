## What a Daily Sales Report Tells a Distributor

For a distribution business, the day's selling is the pulse. It says whether the routes moved stock, whether the new scheme landed, and whether the month is tracking to its target or drifting. An owner who knows his daily number by 7 PM runs a different business from one who learns it two days later when the accountant closes the books.

The number tells you the shape of the day, well past a bare total: which parties bought, which items moved, whether one big invoice carried the day or a hundred small ones did. Read that way, the daily sales report is the first thing an owner should see each evening, the same way he checks what a party owes before giving fresh credit.

## Where the Day's Sales Live in Tally

In Tally Prime, every sale you book is a voucher, and the Sales Register is the report that gathers them. From Gateway of Tally, go to Display More Reports, then Account Books, then Sales Register, and set the period to today. You get every sales voucher of the day, each with its party and value, and the day's total at the foot.

Drill into any voucher and you see the invoice in full: items, quantities, GST, the lot. Change the period to this week or this month and the same register rolls up the wider picture. It is the one report that answers "kitna becha aaj" without any guesswork, provided you are sitting at the machine that runs it.

## What the Owner Actually Decides From It

The daily number drives real decisions before the day is even over.

| The owner sees | The decision it drives |
|---|---|
| Sales well below the daily average | Call the route, check if a salesman is stuck or a party held an order |
| One party carrying the whole day | Watch the concentration, chase the smaller parties who went quiet |
| A slow item that usually moves | Push a scheme or a reminder to the field team before close |
| A strong day near month end | Judge whether the monthly target is in reach and where to push |

None of this waits for the month-end statement. It is a same-day read, which is exactly why it hurts to have it locked on the office desktop while the owner is on the road.
