## What Sundry Debtors Means in Tally

Sundry Debtors is Tally's default group for the parties who owe you money against goods sold on credit. "Sundry" means assorted, so it is the bucket for every customer who buys on terms, whatever their size. For a distributor this is the whole retail book: the 40-shop route in one town, the two big wholesalers who take stock on 45 days, the sub-dealer who pays late every month.

Because it is an asset group, its balance is money that should come into your business in future. It is the exact mirror of the payable side; the relationship between the two is laid out in [creditors vs debtors in Tally](/blog/creditors-vs-debtors-tally/). The one-line version a distributor says out loud: Sundry Debtors is "jinse humein lena hai," the people who owe us.

## How the Group Is Built, Ledger by Ledger

Every retailer you sell to on credit is a separate ledger classified under Sundry Debtors. When you raise a sales invoice, Tally debits that party's ledger by the invoice amount and their outstanding grows. When they pay, you credit the ledger and it shrinks. The rolled-up total of all those ledgers is the group balance, and that is your receivable.

The health of this group depends on bill-wise details being on, so each invoice carries its own reference and its own outstanding clock. Without it, a party ledger shows one lump number and you cannot tell an old unpaid bill from last week's fresh one. With it, the same balance breaks into the actual bills behind it, which is the foundation for both reminders and the [partywise outstanding statement in Tally](/blog/partywise-outstanding-statement-tally/) that most distributors share with their retailers.

## Reading the Receivable Party by Party

The group total is a board number. The operating number is the party-by-party read with ageing on, because that tells you where the risk sits.

Read this way, the sub-dealer at 88 days is the owner-level call and the wholesaler's ₹90,000 overdue is this week's chase, while route retailer A handles himself. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is Sundry Debtors in Tally?**

A: Sundry Debtors is the Tally group that holds the ledgers of all parties who owe you money for goods sold on credit, such as retailers, kirana stores, and sub-dealers. It is an asset group, and its closing balance is your total accounts receivable at any moment. Each customer is a separate ledger under the group.

**Q: How do I see who owes me money in Tally?**

A: Go to Gateway of Tally, then Display More Reports, then Statements of Accounts, then Outstandings, then Receivables for the consolidated figure. Drill into any party to see their bills. Switch on the age-wise view to sort overdue parties into 30/60/90 day buckets so you know who to chase first.

**Q: What is the difference between Sundry Debtors and Sundry Creditors?**

A: Sundry Debtors holds parties who owe you (customers), and its balance is an asset. Sundry Creditors holds parties you owe (suppliers), and its balance is a liability. Debtors is money coming in; creditors is money going out. A distributor runs both groups at once inside one Tally company.

**Q: Can I see my party outstanding on my phone?**

A: Yes. Takkada reads your Sundry Debtors ledgers and open bills from Tally and shows party-wise outstanding with ageing on mobile. Because it stays in two-way sync with Tally, the figure on the phone matches the office desktop, so an owner or salesman can check a party's balance from home or the field before taking the next order.

**Q: Why is my sundry debtors balance wrong?**

A: Common causes are duplicate customer ledgers splitting one party across two names, a receipt posted On Account instead of against the specific bill it settles, or bill-wise details being off so the ledger shows only a lump total. Keep one clean ledger per customer with bill-wise on, and match every receipt to its invoice.
